**Verdict key.**
- **Consistent**: matches independent sources.
- **Minor difference**: an AH-to-CE span, a spelling, OCR, or a small detail.
- **Inconsistent**: reliable sources say otherwise; what they say is given.
- **Disputed**: sources disagree among themselves; each version is given.
- **Unverified**: no reliable source was found or opened; what was tried is said.
- **Opinion**: not checkable.

Source keys (S-…) are resolved to URLs in §6. Calendar conversions use the tabular Islamic calendar, Julian dates, ±1–2 days (calc). For example, 7 AH = 11 May 628 – 30 Apr 629; 8 AH = 1 May 629 – 19 Apr 630; 9 AH = 20 Apr 630 – 8 Apr 631; 10 AH = 9 Apr 631 – 28 Mar 632; 265 AH = 3 Sep 878 – 22 Aug 879.

### Headline findings
1. **Persia: the "missing" source exists.** Arpee's cited source, "Johanianz (New Julfa, 1881)", is Ter-Hovhaneants, *History of New Julfa*, vol. 2 (1881). On pp. 147–153 it prints an Armenian "authenticated copy of the covenant of the Prophet of the Arabs, given to the nation of the Christians or Armenians in the fourth year of the Hijra". It ends with the Monday date and "Mahamad Jafer ... one of the notable imams" confirming it against the original.
   - Dadoyan's "No such document exists in this book" is wrong.
   - Morrow's claims that the covenant "survives only in English translation" and that Arpee may have mis-referenced it are also wrong. [A; page images re-checked by me]
2. **Najran: Hamidullah calls the texts fabrications.** Morrow presents Hamidullah's reprint as a milestone. Yet Hamidullah, in the *Majmūʿat al-wathāʾiq* itself, prints the two Seert texts with the statement "there is not the slightest doubt that these two texts are fabrications" (5th ed., docs 96–97). Morrow does not mention this. [B; re-checked]
3. **Three of Morrow's quotations omit material that cuts against his case:**
   - Ibn Ishaq says the twenty Christians who came to Mecca came "**from Abyssinia**". Morrow's ellipsis removes this, then asserts a pre-621 Najran delegation (p. 112).
   - Arpee calls the underlying Sinai charter "(though by modern criticism pronounced a forgery)". The ellipsis on p. 99 removes this.
   - Scher's epigraph (p. 109) drops Scher's supporting reference to *Al-Machriq* 1909. [me]
4. **Classical sources record one undated Najran treaty, not two.** That treaty has 2,000 robes, five witnesses and the scribe 'Abd Allah b. Abi Bakr or 'Ali. Morrow's separate "9 AH" and "10 AH" treaties (from Qureshi) are two recensions of the same text. Modern dating of the deputation ranges from 630 (Watt; Shahid, EI2) to 10/631–632 (Schmucker, EI2). [me]
5. **Plain date errors:**
   - Turkish Republic "1916" (actually 1923).
   - Caliphate abolished "1922" (actually 1924).
   - Ja'far's return "626" (actually 7/628).
   - Khaybar "629" (actually 628).
   - Armenian deportation "1606, 150,000" (actually 1604–1605; 300,000–400,000 deported overall).
   - Arpee "1948" (actually 1946; he died in 1947).
   - Chronicle published "five years after" Scher's death (actually four: 1915 to 1919).
   - The *Late Antiquity* page Morrow cites contains no "999/1000 CE" source.

## 3. Discrepancies ranked by significance

1. **Hamidullah's verdict suppressed (p. 138).** Morrow cites Hamidullah's reprint as a step toward recognition. Hamidullah prints the two Seert texts (docs 96–97) with: "there is not the slightest doubt that these two texts are fabrications". His preface says the covenants to Christians and Magians were included "as a specimen of forgery and fabrication". Zein & El-Wakil (2020), who favour the covenants, also note that Hamidullah "expressed his misgivings". (S-HAM, S-ZE20)
2. **Persia: Dadoyan was wrong and Morrow missed the source (pp. 99, 102–103, 107).** The covenant is printed in Armenian in Ter-Hovhaneants, *History of New Julfa* II (1881), pp. 147–153, exactly as Arpee said. This:
   - refutes Dadoyan's "No such document exists in this book";
   - makes Morrow's "survives only in English translation" and his referencing-error hypothesis wrong;
   - shifts the evidential question to the Armenian text's origin. It is presented as an "authenticated copy" with an imam's confirmation, but no older witness, Persian or Arabic, has been identified.

   Arpee's own text calls the underlying Sinai charter "by modern criticism pronounced a forgery". Morrow's p. 99 ellipsis removes that. (S-TH, S-ARP)
3. **Ibn Ishaq misquotation (p. 112).** "From Abyssinia" is cut from Ibn Ishaq's account of the twenty Christians in Mecca. The resulting "first [Najran] delegation ... prior to 621 CE" rests on a reading Ibn Ishaq himself doubts. (S-II)
4. **Two Najran treaties (9 AH and 10 AH) (p. 113).** The classical sources (Ibn Sa'd, Abu Yusuf, Abu 'Ubayd, al-Baladhuri) preserve one undated treaty in several recensions. Morrow's two "treaties" via Qureshi are versions of it. The deputation's date is itself disputed: 630 (Watt, Shahid) against 10 AH (Schmucker, EI2).
5. **Internal impossibility of the witness defence (pp. 115–118).** Morrow dates the covenant to the Prophet's last year. Yet he defends Sa'd b. Mu'adh (d. 5/627) as a witness by placing the covenant "after the Battle of the Trench". He also says Ja'far returned in "626", when EI2 gives 7/628. On the EI2 dates, no single date fits Sa'd, Ja'far, Abu Hurayra (Muslim from 7/628–9) and Mu'awiya (Muslim from 8/630) together. The covenant's own source (the Chronicle) frames the visit c. 628–629. (S-EI2-B, S-PO13)
6. **Political dates (p. 110).** Turkish Republic "1916" (actually 1923); caliphate abolished "1922" (actually 1924; the sultanate fell in 1922). (S-EI-AMII)
7. **Misused citation (p. 114).** "A second Yemeni source from the year 999/1000 CE" is not on the cited *Late Antiquity* p. 753. Later Christian presence is still independently attested: al-Hadi's 897 treaty; bishops in the 9th–10th centuries (Shahid, EI2); Ibn al-Mujāwir in the 13th century. (S-LA, S-EI-NAJ)
8. **'Umar's expulsion (p. 114).** Morrow casts Hitti's statement as biased. Every classical source opened reports the expulsion, and EI2 says "most" of Najran's Christians went to al-Najraniyya near Kufa. Morrow is right only that some Christians stayed (Shahid). The suggestion that only the hierarchs were exiled has no source support.
9. **Scher misrepresented (p. 109).** Scher gave reasons (variant copies across communities; faulty language) and a reference (Cheikho, *Al-Machriq* 1909). Morrow's epigraph removes the reference and then says Scher gave no argument.
10. **New Julfa (p. 99).** "1606 ... over 150,000" should be 1604–05, with 300,000–400,000 deported overall and about 10,000 in New Julfa. Abbas I did not build the surviving cathedral (1653–64, under Abbas II).
11. **Arpee "1948" (p. 103).** The book appeared in 1946 and Arpee died in 1947. The book's own p. 99 says 1946.
12. **Publication of the Chronicle (pp. 109, 138).** It appeared four years, not five, after Scher's death. Parts I–II.1 appeared in his lifetime (1908–1911). Bar Hebraeus's summary was printed in 1877, so "first published ... 1919" holds only for the full text.
13. **Ja'far al-Sadiq "(711–36)"** (Arpee's figures, repeated uncorrected); Mu'awiya in Armenia "650" (actually 652/653); "Jamal Pasha" book-burning (Shi'i tradition names al-Jazzar; rests on snippets).
14. **Witness list in Part II (p. 300).** Three of Scher's names are omitted and two merged.
15. **Minor:**
   - Khaybar 629;
   - CUP "1906";
   - Thévenot's dates;

   - "Bowerstock";

## 4. Scholarly assessment of authenticity

### 4A. Covenant with the Christians of Persia (Arpee 1946; Ter-Hovhaneants 1881)

**Positions found.**
- **Morrow (2013):** authenticity "cannot be disproved"; possibly the original of the Sinai text.
- **Dadoyan (2011):** "highly dubious yet intriguing". Her stated basis, that the document is not in the cited book or the archives, is half refuted for the book; the archive claim is unverified. Her "cannot be established" refers to the oath traditions generally. [A, GB]
- **Arpee (1946):** he reproduces the document. He records the Ja'far authentication, but describes the Sinai charter it reproduces as "by modern criticism pronounced a forgery". [A]
- **Hamidullah:** includes "the Prophetic covenants with the Armenian Christians" in the *Majmūʿa* (pp. 556–560 per Zein & El-Wakil). His general verdict on covenants with Christians is "a specimen of forgery". Whether his Armenian item is this text was not checked. [me]
- **Sellam's review (*AJIS* 2015):** does not assess the Persia covenant. [A]
- No other scholarly verdict was found.

**Strongest evidence for (early origin or authenticity).**
- A printed Armenian text (New Julfa, 1881) exists, explicitly framed as an "authenticated copy" of a covenant given "in the fourth year of the Hijra". It records confirmation by "Mahamad Jafer ... one of the notable imams". This shows a real New Julfa tradition, not a 1946 invention.
- An older Armenian tradition of a written grant from "Mahmet" survives. Kirakos (13th c.) reports a contract with Armenia: four dram, wheat, a saddlebag, a hair rope and gloves.
- Kirakos also records a documented 8th-century petition (John of Odzun to Hisham) securing similar freedoms.

**Strongest evidence against (later composition).**
- No Arabic or Persian text and no manuscript has been identified. The earliest attested form is the 1881 printing, and Arpee says only that a 17th-century shah gave it "in copy".
- The text is avowedly a translation and is "strikingly similar" to the Sinai covenant, which Arpee himself reports modern criticism calls a forgery.
- Shi'i features that Morrow himself notes:
  - the ruler called "Imam";
  - "blessings of God rest upon him and his posterity";
  - temporary marriage;
  - authentication by an imam.

  These fit a Shi'i (Safavid-era) milieu of transmission. Morrow reads them as original; that is an inference either way.
- The date (4 AH) predates any Muslim contact with Persia's Armenians.
- The cited 8th-century evidence does not mention this document.

**Strongest evidence for authenticity, or for an authentic core.**
- A real treaty between Muhammad and Najran is solidly attested in the classical sources: Ibn Sa'd, Abu Yusuf, Abu 'Ubayd and al-Baladhuri, with five named witnesses, and a delegation in Ibn Ishaq.
- The Seert exordium's details overlap with those sources: a mosque visit; a bishop called 'Isa (Bar Hebraeus: "Jesus") with the Sayyid; a delegation of tens of riders.
- Bar Hebraeus (13th c.) independently summarizes a diploma to the Najran Sayyid and bishop with the same 4/12 tax structure. The tradition was therefore known outside the Chronicle.
- El-Wakil argues that the shared anachronisms across covenants held by different communities, without evidence of borrowing, point to common authentic exemplars.
- The provenance note is specific: a register found in 265/878–9 with Habib, former curator of the "house of philosophy"; yellowed ox-hide with the Prophet's seal.

**Strongest evidence against.**
- No Islamic source preserves this text or anything close. El-Wakil concedes this is "certainly perplexing".
- The terms differ in kind from the classical Najran treaty: a per-head 4/12-dirham tax and clergy exemptions, against a collective tribute of 2,000 robes.
- The witness list cannot be reconciled with a single date: Sa'd b. Mu'adh d. 5/627 against Abu Hurayra (7/628–9) and Ja'far (7/628). Mu'awiya, a Muslim only from 8/630, appears as scribe.
- The exordium groups Hunayn (8/630) with the Qaynuqa', Nadir and Qurayza episodes (2–5 AH).
- The document surfaced in 878, amid 'Abbasid-era disputes over dhimmi taxation, and was recorded in a chronicle composed in the 10th century or later.
- Its length and repetitiveness (Wood).
- Its "incorrect" language (Scher).
- Its own frame dates the visit to c. 628–629, which conflicts with other datings.
- Its role in the Chronicle's arguments for clerical tax exemption (Wood).
