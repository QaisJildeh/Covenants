# V6b: Morrow (2013), Chapter 15 "The Covenants in Context" (printed pp. 333–352): fact-check of timeline rows and themes E–K

**Book:** John Andrew Morrow, *The Covenants of the Prophet Muhammad with the Christians of the World* (Angelico/Sophia Perennis, 2013).
**Scope:** Chapter 15 only, covering:
- the extraction's §2 timeline rows T76–T208;
- its §4 claims C73–C174 (themes E–K);
- the Chapter 15 items in its §7.

Biographical lifespans are left to the lifespan-table agent and listed at the end of §1.

**Inputs:**
- Extraction: `scratchpad/extract/08_ch13_ch17.md`.
- Book text: `scratchpad/book/chunks/08_ch13_ch17.txt` (book pages 333–352 = PDF pages 358–377). Every book quotation below was checked against this text.

**How the work was done:**
- The lead agent covered early Islam, the Pact and Treaty of 'Umar, the early caliphs, the Ottomans and Mamluks, and the close of the chapter.
- Helper A covered al-Andalus, Granada and the Moriscos.
- Helper C covered the conduct of other powers, law and civilisation, and the Qur'an and hadith citations.
- Working files are in `scratchpad/v6b_work/`: part_M_early_ottoman.md, part_A_andalus.md and part_C_powers_law.md. Downloads are in dlA/, dlB/, dlC/ and dlM/.
- Rows taken from helper files keep their original id in brackets, e.g. [A16] or [C7a].

**Evidence rules:**
- Every verdict rests on a page or full text that was actually opened (WebFetch, or a curl download of the full text). Search-engine snippets were never used as evidence.
- "OL search-inside" (Helper C) means verbatim OCR excerpts returned by the Open Library full-text API for a lending-only book. These are verbatim but partial.
- Wikipedia is marked "(lead)" wherever it appears, and is used only as a lead or for uncontroversial dates.
- Earlier verification files were reused and cited by name, for example V5 for the Jerusalem surrender dates, V1b for the campaign counts, and Part B for the coinage.

**Verdict labels:** Consistent / Minor difference / Inconsistent / Disputed among sources / Unverified / Opinion.
Before any item was marked Inconsistent, OCR error and AH-to-CE conversion were ruled out and a second source was sought.

**Row keys:** T = extraction §2 timeline row; C = extraction §4 claim; S7-n = extraction §7 item.

## 3. Discrepancies ranked by significance

Ranking criteria: (1) how far the error changes the historical picture or the chapter's argument; (2) whether a quotation or source is misattributed; (3) whether it is a plain factual slip; (4) minor dating, wording or OCR issues.

### Tier 1: substantive errors that bear on the chapter's argument

1. **The Capitulations' dates and scope are wrong (p. 349; T198).** The book says they "remained current from 1517 to their abolishment by the Treaty of Lausanne in 1923" and granted "custody of certain holy places".
   - İnalcık (TDV "İmtiyâzât") dates the first Ottoman capitulation to c. 1352 (Genoa); the earliest surviving text is of 1387.
   - In 1517 Selim I only *renewed* the Mamluk grants.
   - The first ratified French capitulation is 1569; the 1536 draft was never confirmed.
   - The holy-places privileges came in 1604, 1690 and 1740.
   - The Ottomans abolished the capitulations unilaterally on 8 Sept 1914; Lausanne (24 July 1923) made the Allies accept that.
2. **The persecution blamed on the "Arian" Visigoths was carried out by Catholic Visigoths (p. 341; T131) [C9a–C9c].**
   - *Encyclopaedia Judaica* ("Spain") and the Visigothic Code put the forced baptisms (613), the bans on circumcision and the Sabbath (653), the confiscations, the punitive taxes and the enslavement (694) after Reccared's conversion in 587/589.
   - Ziegler (1930) and Bachrach (via OL search-inside) describe the Arian kings as comparatively tolerant.
   - The book's later praise of the Catholic Church (p. 350) sits awkwardly with this misattribution.
3. **"Only … in 1923" did non-Muslim rights begin to be encroached upon (p. 349; T201; S7-17).**
   - Britannica: the Hamidian massacres of 1894–96 killed hundreds of thousands; Adana 1909 about 20,000; the Armenian Genocide of 1915–16 600,000 to more than 1,000,000.
   - The book's own pp. 347–348 and the Karabell sentence it quotes immediately afterwards also contradict "only … in 1923".
4. **Two quotations are attributed to Wiegers but are not in Wiegers (pp. 343–344; T142 [A17], T160 [A38]).** Both helper A and the lead agent checked Wiegers 1994 pp. 9–11.
   - Neither "within the space of seven years these generous terms had been broken" nor the Galera sentence is there.
   - On Galera itself: the eyewitness Mármol (II, Bk VIII) and Lea p. 255 give more than 2,400 fighting men killed, more than 400 women and children killed, and 4,500 women and children enslaved. The town was razed and salted.
   - "All its 2,500 inhabitants … slaughtered" matches English Wikipedia, which cites a school textbook.
5. **Several statements about the Morisco expulsion contradict the edicts and the documentary record (p. 345).**
   - *Children (T166) [A44]:* no edict sold children over seven as slaves.
   - *Sequence (T164–T165) [A42]:* the expulsion ran Valencia 1609 → Andalusia, Castile, Aragon and Catalonia 1610 → Murcia and Ricote 1613–14, not "Castile in 1614".
   - *"50,000 died resisting" (T167) [A45]:* this traces to the pro-expulsion writer Aznar Cardona (1612), who says they "died miserably", not "resisting".
   - *"Two-thirds to three-quarters" (T170) [A48]:* this is a contemporary estimate for deportees in North Africa (Lea p. 364), not for the whole Morisco population.
   - *Totals:* the book gives 300,000 and "half a million" (S7-19). Lapeyre counts 272,140; Britannica and Bernabé Pons give 275,000–300,000. The book's Chejne quotation leaves out Chejne's own Valencia figure and Chejne's notes questioning the regional totals.
6. **'Umar's visit to Jerusalem is dated "659 CE" (p. 335; T96; S7-5).** 'Umar died in 644. All the source dates for the surrender fall between 634/5 and 638. "659" is the book's own date for Mu'awiya (T98) and was probably transferred from there.
7. **Battle of the Masts "while governor of Syria under the Caliph 'Umar" (p. 340; T116; S7-6).**
   - Baladhuri says 'Umar *refused* Mu'awiya a naval expedition, and the first expedition came under 'Uthman (AH 28).
   - The battle (655, or AH 31/34) fell in 'Uthman's caliphate.
   - Theophanes names Abu'l-A'war as the fleet commander.
8. **"The Caliph 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Marwan … known for his love of the Christians" (p. 336; T99).** He was governor of Egypt, not caliph (LC authority). Fortescue, whom the book cites elsewhere, records him extorting 100,000 dinars from Patriarch John III by torture, taxing monks and forbidding new monastic vocations (p. 229).
9. **The Prophet's 1,000-casualty total (p. 333; T78).** No source was found for it, and it conflicts with the traditional figures. The Qurayza execution alone is put at 600–900, or 400–900 in Kister's survey (prior check V1b). The campaign count "68" differs from Ibn Ishaq's 65 and Ibn Sa'd's 74.

### Tier 2: plain factual errors (the source contradicts the book)

10. **Granada's population (p. 343; T139) [A13].** The book gives 500,000, split into 100,000, 200,000 and 200,000. Scholars estimate about 300,000 for the whole kingdom before the war (Ladero 1968; Cortés Peña 2009) and about 165,000 by 1502. The city's Moorish population was about 40,000–50,000.
11. **Bayezid II "committed his forces to Cyprus" (p. 342; T137) [A11].** Cyprus was Venetian (1489–1570/71), and Ottoman forces were fighting the Mamluks in 1485–91. Kemal Reis did raid Spain in 1487 (TDV). The 1492 fleet to evacuate Jews (T138) [A12] is not documented in TDV or Britannica.
12. **Pragmática dated "1565" (p. 344; T156) [A30].** It was proclaimed on 1 January 1567, after the 1566 Madrid junta; 1565 was the provincial council. The book also makes a "decree turning children over to priests" the trigger of the Alpujarras revolt (T158) [A36], but no such clause is in the Pragmática.
13. **"On October 12th, 1501, all Arabic and Islamic books in Granada were burned" (p. 343; T147) [A21].** Cisneros burned the books in 1499–1500. 12 October 1501 is the date of a royal order on Arabic books that *exempted* medical works (Wiegers p. 10 n.).
14. **"Eye for an eye … unheard of in Europe" (p. 341; C114) [C6].** Talion is in the Twelve Tables (VIII.2) and Exodus 21:23–25.
15. **Wrong collection and verse reference (S7-15, S7-16).** The "oppresses a Jew or a Christian" hadith is not in Bukhari; the nearest text is Abu Dawud 3052, about any *mu'ahid*, with mixed gradings (C73) [C12a]. The verse cited as "5:72" is 2:62 (C115) [C11a].
16. **The shari'ah as "the inspiration for the Napoleonic Code" (pp. 349–350; T203) [C13a].** Mainstream legal history (Bonfield) traces the Code to Roman law and French customary law. Perron's Maliki translation appeared in 1848–49, after the Code. The Quebec code of 1866 rests on the Custom of Paris.
17. **"Hun" criminal law (p. 349; C116) [C3]** is Sima Qian's description of the **Xiongnu** (*Shiji* 110). The "crushing of bones" is only Yan Shigu's gloss.
18. **"Romans … used to sow with salt" (p. 341; C112) [C2c].** The Carthage salting is a modern myth (Ridley 1986; Warmington 1988). No Mongol case was found.
19. **Slips in the al-Andalus passage (p. 342).** 'Abd al-Rahman I was not "the last Caliph of the Umayyads" (T135) [A9]; Marwan II was. It was the Mongols in 1258, not "the Huns", who razed Baghdad [A8]. The Emirate of Granada did not last "more than seven hundred years" (T134) [A7]; the Nasrids ruled 1238–1492.
20. **Qum as one of 'Umar's garrison towns (p. 334; T82).** Iranica: Qum was largely untouched for 60 years after 644, and Arabs first settled there permanently in 685–96.
21. **Mamluk–French "treaty … in Cairo in 1500" (p. 347; T184).** The documented Mamluk grant to the French and Catalans is of 1507 (IFAO).
22. **The Fojnica ahdname described as "the original" (p. 345; T174).** The displayed document is partly a later renewal: radiocarbon dates the upper part to 1665–1808 (Horvatinčić et al. 2017). The consensus is that the text is authentic and the grant was issued in 1463 (Sekulić).
23. **Lebanon "still almost half Christian" (p. 334; C81).** Statistics Lebanon (US State Dept 2023 report) gives 30.5% of the citizen population.

### Tier 3: misquotation, selective quotation and context omitted

24. **The Treaty of 'Umar's harvest clause is reversed (T106).** The book has "Nothing shall be taken from them **after** their crops have been harvested"; Tabari XII has "**before** their harvest is reaped".
25. **Quotations shortened without ellipsis, changing their force:**
    - Eliot: "… privileges granted by the conqueror **by enormous presents**" (T188).
    - Lybyer: "nine centuries before Selim" dropped; "religion" changed to "creed" (T189).
    - Scher: "probablement **aussi** apocryphe" (T112).
    - Joshel's block quote: condensed, with "in Epirus" dropped (T123–T126) [C1e].
    - Chronicle of Seert: the sentence "they destroyed the fire-temples" is left out of the praise passage (T207).
    - Chejne: the Valencia figure is omitted (T171) [A49].
26. **The JVL/*EJ* quotation on the Pact of 'Umar stops before *EJ*'s next point (C93).** *EJ* goes on: "Recent research … considers many of its provisions to be consistent with the early years of the conquest" (Noth, Cohen).
27. **Quotations whose own context cuts against the use made of them:**
    - Isho'yahb III's praise of the Arabs (T89) comes in a letter rebuking the Christians of Mazun for converting to keep half their property.
    - Fortescue frames the "Bishop of Adiabene" report as part of "legends … made deliberately to persuade the Moslem conqueror" (T90). Fortescue also names the 1630 "Testament of Mohammed" in this context.
    - Wigram's "eternity of dullness, enlivened only by occasional risk of massacre" (C149) is cited as support.
28. **"'Umar … brought Jews back" (p. 338; T111)** conflicts with the Tabari treaty the book prints on p. 337 ("None of the Jews shall reside with them"). The sources disagree (El-Awaisi pp. 58–59).
29. **Selim I "attempted to exterminate all the partisans of the Household of the Prophet" (p. 348).** TDV (Emecen) rejects the 40,000 figure and says the targets were mainly Safavid propagandists.
30. **Nuseibeh custody through "'Ubadah ibn al-Samit" (p. 335).** This is family tradition, and the family's own accounts name Abdullah b. Nusaybah and also point to Saladin's time. The key is physically held by the Joudeh family.

## 4. Contested framings (both sides, brief, cited)

1. **Islamic conquest compared with other powers (pp. 334–341).**
   - *For:* Donner and Avni are reported as finding little archaeological destruction. Avni (abstract): the Islamic conquest "had only a minor effect" on Jerusalem's layout [C, §3]. Many of the Roman, Frankish, Persian and Hun atrocities the book cites are attested in ancient sources (Livy, Josephus, the Royal Frankish Annals, Sebeos) [C1a–C7].
   - *Against or qualifying:* Several comparison claims are wrong: Arian persecution, Roman salting, Hun law and talion. The book sets Islamic *norms* (Qur'an, covenants) against other powers' *worst practice*, while omitting Islamic-side episodes recorded in its own sources. These include Qurayza (V1b), Mazun's conversions under fiscal pressure (Isho'yahb III), the Chronicle of Seert's note that the conquerors destroyed fire-temples, and 'Abd al-'Aziz's extortions (Fortescue). Modern figures are often self-reported or partisan (Scheidel; Thompson) [C, §3].
2. **The Crusades as extermination (p. 335).** *Book (via Mamdani):* "the point of the Crusades was not to convert Muslims but to exterminate them." *Britannica:* the objectives were "to check the spread of Islam, to retake control of the Holy Land …, to conquer pagan areas, and to recapture formerly Christian territories". (The chapter does not discuss the 1099 sack of Jerusalem, so it was not checked.)
3. **Authenticity of the Pact of 'Umar (pp. 336–339).**
   - *Forgery or late:* de Goeje and Caetani ("invention of a later age", Arnold p. 57); Tritton ("no one knew what the covenant of 'Umar was", p. 12); Bernard Lewis ("can hardly be authentic", via Wikipedia lead).
   - *Early roots:* Noth (stipulations rooted in the conquest treaties); Cohen (a petition-and-decree form that medieval Muslims would accept as authentic); Levy-Rubin (formalised in the early 'Abbasid period out of earlier surrender agreements and pre-Islamic models). Sources: *EJ*; CUP description.
   - The book reports only the first group, and takes from JVL/*EJ* the half of the entry that suits it.
4. **Authenticity of the Tabari Jerusalem treaty (p. 339).** The book says "historians approve" it.
   - *Fabrication:* Caetani (via Tritton p. 114); Goitein and Hitti (via El-Awaisi).
   - *Reliable kernel:* Gil (via El-Awaisi).
   - *Undecided:* Kennedy (2007, p. 92), "we cannot be sure" (prior check C_islamic_chronology).
5. **The Seert covenants (pp. 338–339).** Morrow attributes Scher's verdict to "prejudice". Scher (PO 13 p. 623 n. 1) calls the Najran covenant and the 'Umar edict "deux faux" (two forgeries). Wood (2013, 2021) and Wilmshurst (2011) also treat them as 'Abbasid-era or later Christian productions (see V5 §4; V0). The defence of authenticity comes mainly from Morrow's circle (V5).
6. **Al-Andalus as a "golden age" (pp. 341–342).**
   - *For:* Menocal 2002; *Convivencia* 1992; Britannica on 'Abd al-Rahman III's "tolerant reign".
   - *Against:* Fernández-Morera 2016 (summarised by R. V. Young in *Modern Age*, a conservative journal). Episodes cited include the Córdoba martyrs (850–859/60, 53 executed), the Granada massacre of 1066 and the Almohad ultimatum of 1148 [A1; A, §3].
7. **The Ottoman "millet system" and Ottoman tolerance (pp. 346–349).**
   - *For:* Bodin's 1606 testimony to four religions tolerated at Pera; Lybyer's account of the Sacred Law protecting Christians; Mehmed II's grants (Gennadios, the 1461 Armenian patriarchate tradition) (Bodin; Lybyer; TDV "Millet").
   - *Qualifying:* Braude's "Foundation Myths of the Millet System" (cited by Ortaylı in TDV "Millet", which notes "dispute" over the dates). Selim I's punishment of Kızılbaş, Selim II's 1568 confiscations, and the 19th–20th-century massacres (TDV; Kermeli; Britannica).
   - The book's own evidence (pp. 347–348) undercuts its claim (p. 349) that encroachment began only in 1923.
8. **Bayezid II's "rescue fleet" of 1492 (p. 342).** It is widely repeated in popular and Turkish public sources (e.g. +972 Magazine). The scholarly encyclopedia articles opened (TDV Bayezid II, TDV Kemal Reis, Britannica) say only that Jews were welcomed; TDV says Kemal Reis carried Andalusian *Muslims* [A12].
9. **The Fojnica ahdname as a "human rights declaration" (p. 345).** The "326 years before 1789 … 485 years before 1948" formula is a modern Bosniak and Turkish public trope. Sekulić calls it "curious, if anachronistic" (*New Lines*, 2022). The book repeats it without comment.
10. **Verden 782 (p. 341).** The numbers are in the annals. Exculpatory readings ("delocati" for "decollati") are generally rejected (Barbero via Wikipedia; Chamberlin), while the scale is debated: Lintzel and Becher versus Bachrach (via Wikipedia, not opened) [C7; C, §3].
11. **The Napoleonic Code (pp. 349–350).** A pro-Maliki-influence article in a Qatar University journal exists but could not be opened. The mainstream view is Roman plus customary law (Bonfield). The book's wording treats the mainstream as a fringe "attempt" [C13a].
