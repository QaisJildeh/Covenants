## B1. Selim I, Baghdad and "Shimon IV" (1517)

**Finding as reported.** The book (p. 177, also p. 180) says that in 1517 Selim I took the Assyrian covenant from the patriarch "Shimon IV" at Baghdad. Against this:
- Baghdad was Safavid from 1508 to 1534, and Selim never went there.
- Shem'on IV died in 1497, and the patriarch in 1517 was Shem'on VI.
- Malech's own list (Malech p. 314) agrees.

**What the book says.**
- p. 177 (PDF 202), block quotation of Malech: "In 1517 Sultan Selim came from Constantinople to Bag[h]dad, and it is said, that Shimon the fourth, the patriarch, visited the sultan and showed him the precious document. And the sultan said that it was so valuable, that it ought to be preserved in the royal treasury of Constantinople. The patriarch had to give it up, but Selim gave him a copy of the document, signed by himself. The copy is kept by the patriarch; it is still extant—the original is in Constantinople. (Malech 221)"
- p. 178, the author's own endorsement: "As to the claim that it was Selim I who, in 1517, insisted that the Covenant ... be preserved in the Treasury in Istanbul, it makes perfect chronological sense."
- p. 180: "what happened, then, to the certified copy left behind by Sultan Selim I? Well, according to George David Malech, the original was taken to Istanbul in 1517."
- Accuracy note: the book quotes Malech accurately. The Baghdad and "Shimon the fourth" details appear in that quotation on p. 177, which p. 178 endorses. p. 180 repeats only the 1517 transfer to Istanbul.

**What the primary sources say.**
- **Malech** (1910). Item https://archive.org/details/historyofsyriann00male; I checked the page images (n246, n336, n339).
  - p. 221 matches the book's quotation. The original reads "1517 Sultan Selim came from Constantinople to Bagdad, and it is said …", with no source given.
  - p. 311: "Patriarchs: Elias IV, 1405-1433; Shimon IV, 1433-1472; Shimon V, 1472-1503."
  - p. 314: "Patriarchs: Elias V, 1503-1504; Shimon VI, 1504-1538; …"
- **Iranica, "Iraq iv. Safavid period"** (Wayback: http://web.archive.org/web/20261003041726/https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iraq-iv-safavid-period/): "After Shah Esmāʿil I took Baghdad from the Āq Qoyunlu … in 1508, the city and its environs remained in Safavid hands until the Ottomans retook the area in 1534."
- **TDV, "Selim I"** (https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/selim-i):
  - It traces Selim's 1517–18 movements: Gaza "8 Zilhicce 922 (2 Ocak 1517)"; Cairo (entry 15 February [1517]); Damascus; Aleppo "11 Safer 924 (22 Şubat 1518)"; then the return to Istanbul.
  - The article never mentions Baghdad ("Bağdat": 0 occurrences).
  - Süleyman entered Baghdad on 30 November [1534] (TDV, "Süleyman I").
- **Wilmshurst, *The Martyred Church* (2011)** (https://archive.org/details/the-martyred-church; djvu text):
  - "Shem'on IV Basidi, who died in 1497 (his epitaph has been preserved in the monastery of Rabban Hormizd …)"
  - The patriarch list gives "Shem'on VI (1504-38)".
  - "The last Nestorian patriarch to reside habitually in Baghdad was Makkikha II (1257-65)."

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** All three points stand:
- Baghdad was Safavid in 1517.
- Selim's 1517 movements did not include Baghdad.
- The patriarch in 1517 was Shimon VI, by Malech's own list and by Wilmshurst's.

One correction to the wording. In V5's ranked list, "Shem'on IV died in 1497, and Malech's own list on p. 314 says so" is inaccurate:
- Malech's list (p. 311) gives Shimon IV as 1433–1472.
- The 1497 date is Wilmshurst's, and Wilmshurst uses a different numbering (he inserts a "Shem'on III").
- The two lists agree on what matters here: Shimon VI was patriarch from 1504 to 1538.

## B2. Date and witnesses of the Assyrian covenant

**Finding as reported.**
- The covenant the book reprints (Ch. 12, pp. 313–314, from Malech 1910) is dated "4th year of the Hegira".
- The book (p. 183) dates it "two years before" 630–632.
- Its witness list includes Hamza (killed 3 AH) and Ja'far b. Abi Talib (in Abyssinia until 7 AH).

**What the book says.**
- p. 314 (PDF 339), last line of the reprint: "This agreement is written down by Moavijah Ben Sofian, according to the dictates of Mohammed, the messenger of God, in the 4th year of the Hegira in the city of Medina."
- The signatures (p. 314) include "Hamza Ibn Almulabb … Jafar Ibn Abu Talib". The author's bracketed transliteration renders these as "Hamzah ibn al-Muttalib … Jafar ibn Abi Talib".
- p. 183: "Mu'awiyyah did indeed act as Muhammad's scribe during the final two years of his life: from the year 630 to the year 632 CE. However, the Covenant of the Prophet Muhammad with the Assyrian Christians dates from two years before."

**What the primary sources say.**
- **Malech** pp. 229–230 (archive.org djvu text) has the same colophon: "…in the 4th year of the Hegira in the city of Medina." By the tabular calendar, 1 Muharram 4 AH falls in mid-June 625, so 4 AH = 625/626 CE.
- **Ibn Ishaq, tr. Guillaume** (https://archive.org/details/GuillaumeATheLifeOfMuhammad; djvu text):
  - p. 375: Wahshi's account of killing Hamza at Uhud.
  - p. 389: "The battle was fought on the sabbath in mid-Shawwal". The next section is headed "THE DAY OF AL-RAJI', A.H. 3".
  - p. 526: Ja'far is among those "who stayed in Abyssinia until he sent 'Amr b. Umayya … to fetch them back … and who ultimately rejoined him in Khaybar after al-Hudaybiya". Khaybar is headed "A.H. 7" (p. 510).

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.**
- 4 AH is about 4–5 years before 630, not two.
- At 4 AH Hamza was already dead (Uhud, Shawwal 3 AH).
- Ja'far was still in Abyssinia; he rejoined the Prophet in 7 AH.

## B3. Sanjian and Samuel of Ani

**Finding as reported.**
- Sanjian calls the edicts of the Prophet, 'Umar and 'Ali to the Armenians fabricated and without historical foundation; the book (pp. 199–200) cites him only for "attributed".
- The book's quotation of Samuel of Ani (via Thomson) reads "ordered tax to be taken" where the source reads "ordered no tax".

**What the book says.**
- p. 199 (PDF 224): "Avedis K. Sanjian, for example, speaks of '[t]he Firman attributed to the Prophet Mohammed, confirming the rights of the Armenians in the Holy Places of Jerusalem' (Narkiss 11), '[t]he Firman attributed to Omar Ibn al-Khattab [sic]' (11), and '[t]he Firman attributed to Caliph 'Ali' (13)."
- p. 200 (PDF 225; checked on the page image): "…one cord of hair, and one gauntlet. But from the priests, nobles and cavalry he ordered tax to be taken, (qtd. Thomson, 'Muhammad' 843)". The word "no" is absent on the printed page, so this is not an OCR error.

**What the primary sources say.**
- **Sanjian**, *The Armenian Communities in Syria under Ottoman Dominion* (Harvard 1965), p. 169 (https://archive.org/details/Sanjian1965ACSOD; djvu text):
  - "Equally unauthentic are the edicts which the Armenian patriarch of Jerusalem is alleged to have received not only from Caliph Umar I but also from the Prophet Muhammad and the orthodox Caliph Ali. All of these documents, which must have been fabricated to support rival claims to the dominical sanctuaries, have no historical foundation."
  - n. 2 (pp. 351–352) speaks of "The apocryphal charter issued to the Armenians by the Prophet Muhammad".
- **Thomson**, "Muhammad and Islam in Armenian tradition", p. 843 (https://archive.org/details/thomsonmuhammadislamarmlit; djvu text): "…one cord of hair, and one gauntlet (45). But from the priests, nobles and cavalry he ordered no tax to be taken."
- Not checked: Narkiss p. 11, the passage the book actually cites (not available). It may well say "attributed". The point stands that Sanjian's own monograph goes further.

**Audit verdict: Confirmed** (both parts).

## B4. Date of the Sharif of Mecca's decree

**Finding as reported.** The decree the book dates to 1917 (p. 201) is dated 18 Rajab 1336, which is 29 April 1918.

**What the book says.** p. 201 (PDF 226): "In 1917, the Sharif of Mecca, al-Husayn ibn 'Ali, issued an edict which was aimed at all Muslims." The quoted text follows: "You are asked to protect and take great care of the entire Armenian, Jacobite, community … 'Whoever takes even a string from them, I will be his adversary on the Day of Judgment'…"

**What the primary source says.**
- **Armenian National Institute** (https://www.armenian-genocide.org/Affirmation.4/current_category.1/affirmation_detail.html):
  - The entry is headed "April 29, 1918".
  - The text is addressed "to … Prince Faisal and Prince Abd al-'Aziz al-Jarba".
  - "This letter is written from Imm Al-Qura (Mecca), on 18 Rajab 1336 [April 29, 1918]".
  - The body matches the book's quotation ("Whosoever takes from them even a rope, I will be his adversary on the day of Judgment").
- **Image of the engraved document** (https://www.armenian-genocide.org/uploads/Affirmation/4.jpg): I read "١٨ رجب ١٣٣٦" on line 3.
- **Tabular conversion:** 18 Rajab 1336 = 29 April 1918, and 18 Rajab 1335 = 10 May 1917.

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** The text the book quotes is the letter of 18 Rajab 1336 (29 April 1918). The letter is addressed to two named princes, not "all Muslims".

Not checked: whether a separate 1917 Sharifian manifesto exists (V5 cites one from Margaryan's abstract). Even if it does, the quoted text is not from it.

## B5. Barakat Ahmad on executions and expulsions

**Finding as reported.**
- The book (p. 41) says: "As far as Ahmad is concerned, no Jews were executed or expelled from Medina during Muhammad's life".
- Barakat Ahmad (*Muhammad and the Jews*, 1979) accepts the banishment of the Banu Nadir and the execution of about 16–17 Qurayza leaders.

**What the book says.** p. 41 (PDF 66): "While not entirely convincing, Syed Barakat Ahmad's (d. 1988 CE) Muhammad and the Jews: A Re-Examination also cast doubts on the credibility of Ibn Ishaq's account … As far as Ahmad is concerned, no Jews were executed or expelled from Medina during Muhammad's life."

**What the primary source says.** Barakat Ahmad, *Muhammad and the Jews: A Re-Examination* (1979). PDF: https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/Library/Barakat,%20A%20-%20Muhammad%20and%20the%20Jews.pdf (page images checked).
- p. 60: "Sūrat al-Ḥashr, which was revealed after the Battle of Uḥud, in the fourth year of the Hijrah and deals with the banishment of the B. al-Naḍīr from Medina, refer to their expulsion as 'the first exile'."
- p. 60: "While the Qur'ān mentions the B. al-Naḍīr's expulsion and the B. Qurayẓah's punishment, it does not refer to the B. Qaynuqā''s expulsion".
- p. 91: "The leaders, Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab, Ka'b b. Asad, Nabbāsh b. Qays and Ghazzāl b. Samaw'al were executed by 'Alī and Zubayr … The culpable leadership of a tribe of 600 to 900 men … would not normally exceed sixteen, or seventeen".
- What Ahmad does deny is the Qaynuqa' expulsion: "the B. Qaynuqā' were not expelled during the Apostle's lifetime".

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** Ahmad accepts the Nadir banishment from Medina and the execution of the Qurayza leaders. He denies only the Qaynuqa' expulsion.

## B6. The "poisoned mouthful" and the Banu Nadir

**Finding as reported.**
- The book (p. 39) cites a "poisoned mouthful" against the Banu Nadir before 625.
- In Ibn Ishaq the poisoning is at Khaybar in 7 AH (Guillaume p. 516). The Nadir plot is a dropped rock (Guillaume p. 437).

**What the book says.** p. 39 (PDF 64): "If the Jews of Banu Qaynuqa' opposed Muhammad verbally, the Jews of Banu Nadir initiated a treacherous plot against the Prophet's life. After at least two attempts on the life of the Prophet, including an incident in which Muhammad was as close as a poisoned mouthful away from death, the Commander-in-Chief of the Muslim Community opted to exile the Banu Nadir from Medina in the year 625 CE." No source is given for the poisoning.

**What the primary source says.** Ibn Ishaq, tr. Guillaume (archive.org, as above).
- p. 437 ("THE DEPORTATION OF THE B. AL-NADIR, A.H. 4"): "'Who will go to the top of the house and drop a rock on him … and rid us of him?' … 'Amr b. Jihash b. Ka'b volunteered to do this".
- p. 516, after the conquest of Khaybar: "Zaynab d. al-Harith, the wife of Sallam b. Mishkam prepared for him a roast lamb … she put a lot of poison in it … He took hold of the shoulder and chewed a morsel of it, but he did not swallow it".
- Nuance: Ibn Ishaq's list of rabbis (p. 239) places "Sallam b. Mishkam" under "From B. al-Nadir". So the poisoner was the wife of a Nadir man. The event is still at Khaybar, after the exile.

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** In Ibn Ishaq the Nadir plot before the exile is the dropped rock. The poisoning is at Khaybar (7 AH), after the 625 exile, so the book's chronology is not supported. The Nadir link through Sallam b. Mishkam's wife is real but does not change the date.

## B7. Bracketed clause in Ibn Ishaq p. 672

**Finding as reported.** The book (p. 52) quotes Ibn Ishaq 672 as "Fight everyone in the way of Allah [who attacked you or betrayed you]". Guillaume p. 672 has no such clause.

**What the book says.** p. 52 (PDF 77): "The Prophet exhorted his commander to 'Fight everyone in the way of Allah [who attacked you or betrayed you] and kill those who disbelieve in Allah' (672)."

**What the primary source says.** Guillaume p. 672: "Take it, Ibn 'Auf; fight everyone in the way of God and kill those who disbelieve in God. Do not be deceitful with the spoil; do not be treacherous, nor mutilate, nor kill children."

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** Guillaume has no such clause. The words appear in square brackets, which conventionally mark an editorial insertion, so this is an interpolation rather than a silent change. But it is placed inside the quotation marks, it is not identified as the author's gloss, and it narrows the scope of the command.

## B8. The "twenty-five years of silence" and the Fijar war

**Finding as reported.**
- The book's "twenty-five years of silence" (pp. 6, 12) is contradicted by the sira: Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Sa'd and al-Tabari place Muhammad in Mecca at about 20 (Fijar, Hilf al-fudul), 25 (marriage) and 35 (rebuilding of the Ka'ba).
- Ibn Sa'd quotes him: "I attended it [Fijar] with my uncles and shot arrows".
- The book calls his Fijar participation "spurious" (p. 6).

**What the book says.**
- pp. 5–6 (PDF 28–29): "As to what took place between the encounter with Bahira in 582 CE and Muhammad's marriage to Khadijah (d. 619 CE) in 595 CE, the sources are silent with the exception of spurious allegations that he participated in the Sacrilegious War or Harb al-fijar when he was twenty years of age."
- p. 12 (PDF 35): "There is thus a period of ten years … from the time he was fifteen to the time he was twenty-five, that is unaccounted for. There is also a second period of fifteen years, from the time he was married … until he declared his prophethood, at the age of forty … These two blocks of time represent twenty-five years of silence."

**What the primary sources say.**
- **Guillaume** p. 82: "This war broke out when the apostle was twenty years of age."
- **Ibn Hisham's note 124** (Guillaume pp. 709–710): "When the apostle was 14 or 15 years old … The apostle was present at some of these encounters, his uncles having taken him with them. He used to say that he picked up the arrows which the enemy had shot and gave them to his uncles to shoot."
- **Ibn Hisham's note 126** (p. 711): "At the age of 25 the apostle married Khadija".
- **Guillaume** p. 84: "Quraysh decided to rebuild the Ka'ba when the apostle was thirty-five years of age".
- **Guillaume** pp. 57–58 (Hilf al-fudul): "The apostle of God said, 'I witnessed in the house of 'Abdullah b. Jud'an a covenant…'". No age is given.
- **Ibn Sa'd**, tr. Moinul Haq, vol. 1, p. 143 (https://archive.org/details/kitab-al-tabaqat-al-kabir-ibn-sad-s.-moinul-haq-h.-k.-ghazanfar-1):
  - "I attended it with my uncles and shot arrows there and I do not repent it. He was twenty years old at that time".
  - p. 144: "The oath of al-Fudul was taken after the return of the Quraysh from the battle of al-Fijar, and the Apostle of Allah then was twenty years of age."
  - Married at twenty-five ("married her when he was twenty-five years old").
  - Thirty-five at the Ka'ba rebuilding.
- **Tabari** vol. 6, tr. Watt & McDonald (https://archive.org/details/the-history-of-al-tabari-vol.-6-muhammad-at-mecca-by-tabari-w.-montgomery-watt-m):
  - Marriage at 25 (Hisham b. Muhammad).
  - The Ka'ba rebuilding "fifteen years after the Sacrilegious War, when the Messenger of God was thirty-five".
  - Watt's foreword (p. xxii): these ages are "multiples of five", which "suggests that they are only approximations".

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** The early sira does not leave the two periods silent. It places him at Fijar and the Hilf al-fudul (about age 20) and at the Ka'ba rebuilding (35), though thinly and with rounded ages.

Precision note on Fijar: Ibn Ishaq's own text dates the war by his age but does not itself say he fought. His participation comes from Ibn Hisham's note (at age 14/15) and from Ibn Sa'd (at age 20, in the Prophet's own words). The book gives no source for calling it "spurious".

## B9. Christians and the Medina Constitution

**Finding as reported.** The Medina Constitution (Guillaume pp. 231–233) names Jewish groups but no Christians. The book (p. 33) says Jews and Christians "belonged to the community of believers".

**What the book says.** p. 33 (PDF 58): "the Prophet created an inclusive Community of Believers which included members of all the Abrahamic faiths. In the time of the Prophet, the ahl al-dhimmah, both Jewish and Christian, belonged to the community of believers." The same page adds: "While Donner is right that the Community of Believers was ecumenical and included Jews and Christians…"

**What the primary source says.** Guillaume pp. 231–233, "The covenant between the Muslims and the Medinans and with the Jews":
- "The Jews of the B. 'Auf are one community with the believers (the Jews have their religion and the Muslims have theirs) … The same applies to the Jews of the B. al-Najjar, B. al-Harith, B. Sa'ida, B. Jusham, B. al-Aus, B. Tha'laba…"
- A search of the whole Constitution finds no "Christian", "Nazarene" or *naṣārā*.

**Audit verdict: Confirmed** as a statement about the text: the Constitution names Jewish groups only.

Caveat for fair presentation. The book's sentence is about "the time of the Prophet" in general, and the book draws the inclusion of Christians explicitly from Donner, with whom it agrees on this point. It disputes Donner only on whether Islam had a distinct identity. So this is a contested interpretive claim resting on Donner's thesis, not a misquotation of the Constitution. (V1a's remark that the book "attacks" Donner's thesis on that page is inaccurate on this specific point.)

## B10. Life dates

**Finding as reported.**
- al-Majlisi: the book has "1616–1689" (pp. 2, 6, 7); Iranica and TDV give 1627–1699.
- 'Abd Allah ibn Sallam: the book has "d. 630" (p. 36); EI2 and TDV give 43/663-4.
- "Muhammad al-Kalbi (737–819)" (p. 40): Muhammad b. al-Sa'ib al-Kalbi died 146/763. The span 737–819 belongs to his son Hisham.
- "George Jordac or Jurji Zaydan (1861–1914)" (p. 62) conflates two men.

**What the book says.**
- p. 2: "Muhammad Baqir al-Majlisi (1616-1689 CE)". p. 6: "Majlisi's (1616-1689 CE) version". p. 7: "Majlisi (1616-1689 CE)". Also p. 124: "'Allamah Majlisi (1616-1689 CE)".
- p. 36: "al-Husayn ibn Sallam ibn al-Harith (d. 630 CE), the most learned Jewish rabbi from Yathrib".
- p. 40: "others sources, such as Muhammad al-Kalbi (737-819 CE), make no mention of the Banu Qaynuqa' incident (Scholler 26-27)". The bibliography entry is Schöller, "Sira and Tafsir. Muhammad al-Kalbi on the Jews of Medina".
- p. 62: "As George Jordac or Jurji Zaydan (1861-1914 CE) has eloquently expressed…". The bibliography entry is "Jordac, George. The Voice of Human Justice. Trans. M. Fazal Haq. Qum: Ansariyan, 1990."

**What the sources say.**
- **Majlisi:**
  - Iranica, "Majlesi, Mohammad-Baqer" (Wayback: http://web.archive.org/web/20261019221009/https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/majlesi-mohammad-baqer/) gives "(b. 1627; d. 1699 or 1700)". It also notes that "Most sources agree on 1037/1627-28 as his date of birth (… the hijra year 1027/1618 in Amin … seems to be a misprint)".
  - TDV (https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/meclisi-muhammed-bakir): "1037'de (1627) İsfahan'da doğdu … 1110 (1698-99) veya (1111/1699-1700) yılında İsfahan'da vefat etti".
- **Ibn Sallam:** TDV, "Abdullah b. Selâm" (https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/abdullah-b-selam): "(ö. 43/663-64) … Muâviye'nin halifeliği sırasında Medine'de vefat etmiştir". EI2 was not consulted (paywall).
- **The two Kalbis:**
  - TDV, "Kelbî, Muhammed b. Sâib" (https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/kelbi-muhammed-b-saib): "(ö. 146/763)". He fought at Dayr al-Jamajim "(82/701)", so he cannot have been born in 737.
  - TDV, "Kelbî, Hişâm b. Muhammed" (https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/kelbi-hisam-b-muhammed): "Muhtemelen 120 (738) yılında Kûfe'de doğdu", "(ö. 204/819 [?])".
- **Jordac and Zaydan:**
  - TDV, "Corcî Zeydân" (https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/corci-zeydan): "Corcî b. Habîb Zeydân (1861-1914)".
  - George Jordac: English Wikipedia (tertiary; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Jordac) and Wikidata Q6414987 both give "(1931 – 2014)", Lebanese, author of *The Voice of Human Justice*, which is the book the author cites.

**Audit verdict: Confirmed** (all four):
- Majlisi's dates are wrong by about a decade at both ends.
- Ibn Sallam died in 43/663-4, not 630.
- 737–819 are approximately Hisham's dates, not his father Muhammad's.
- Jordac (1931–2014) and Zaydan (1861–1914) are two different people.

## B11. Reception

**Finding as reported.**
- Reynolds, *First Things* (Feb 2014), "Briefly Noted", calls the covenants late forgeries and says the earliest Sinai copies are 16th-century.
- El-Wakil's 2016 abstract in the *Journal of Islamic Studies* calls forgery "the accepted understanding".
- Monferrer-Sala, *Mediterranea* 11 (2026), finds Morrow's case "more persuasive as advocacy than as a decisive demonstration of authenticity".
- Amar Sellam, who reviewed the book for AJISS, is a listed Covenants Initiative signatory.

**What the primary sources say.**
- **Reynolds** (https://firstthings.com/briefly-noted-59/; page labelled "February 2014 Issue"):
  - "Forged by Christians intent on proving to their Muslim overlords that the Prophet himself had guaranteed their well-being and the preservation of their property, they are all quite late. The earliest copies of 'the covenant of the Prophet with the monks of Mt. Sinai' date to the sixteenth century".
  - Also: "his interest in arguing for the authenticity of these covenants is not unlike the interest of those who forged them in the first place."
- **El-Wakil**, JIS 27:3 (2016), doi 10.1093/jis/etw027. Abstract via the OpenAlex API (https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1093/jis/etw027): "Morrow brought these covenants together … arguing against the accepted understanding that these are forgeries." El-Wakil himself argues for authenticity.

## B12. The p. 112 concession and the early Muslim Najran treaty

**Finding as reported.**
- On p. 112 the book concedes that the Najran covenant "appears to be completely absent in the classical Islamic sources" and that "most scholars dismiss it as dubious".
- The early Muslim sources (Abu Yusuf, Abu 'Ubayd, Ibn Sa'd, al-Baladhuri) give a short tribute treaty (2,000 robes, a loan of arms, a riba clause), not the long covenant.

**What the book says.** p. 112 (PDF 137): "The strongest argument that can be made against the Covenant of the Prophet Muhammad with the Christians of Najran resides in the fact that mention of this document appears to be completely absent in the classical Islamic sources which have survived to the present. It is on this basis that most scholars dismiss it as dubious. However, when viewed in a broader context, such a position seems eminently reactionary and intransigent."

**What the primary sources say.**
- **Abu Yusuf**, *Kitab al-Kharaj* (https://shamela.ws/book/26333/88 and /89, printed pp. 84–85). It gives the copy "التي في أيديهم" (the one in their hands):
  - "على ألفي حلة … في كل رجب ألف حلة وفي كل صفر ألف حلة" (2,000 robes: 1,000 each Rajab and 1,000 each Safar)
  - "وعليهم عارية ثلاثين درعا وثلاثين فرسا وثلاثين بعيرا" (they are to lend 30 coats of mail, 30 horses and 30 camels)
  - "ومن أكل ربا من ذي قبل فذمتي منه بريئة" (whoever henceforth takes usury, my protection is withdrawn from him)
  - "جوار الله وذمة محمد … على أموالهم وأنفسهم وأرضهم وملتهم … وبيعهم … لا يغير أسقف من أسيقفيته ولا راهب من رهبانيته" (the protection of God and the dhimma of Muhammad over their property, persons, land, religion and churches; no bishop is to be moved from his see, nor monk from his monastic office)
- **Abu 'Ubayd**, *Kitab al-Amwal* no. 517 (OpenITI: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0225AH/master/data/0224QasimIbnSallam/0224QasimIbnSallam.Amwal/0224QasimIbnSallam.Amwal.ShamAY0034007-ara1):
  - Same terms: "ألفي حلة … عارية ثلاثين فرسا، وثلاثين بعيرا، وثلاثين درعا … على أن لا ياكلوا الربا" (2,000 robes; a loan of 30 horses, 30 camels and 30 coats of mail; on condition that they do not consume usury)
  - Plus protection of "بيعهم ورهبانيتهم وأساقفتهم" (their churches, their monks and their bishops).
  - No. 518 says 'Umar expelled them because "أصابوا الربا" (they took usury).
- **Ibn Sa'd**, tr. Moinul Haq, vol. 1, pp. 341–342 and 419:
  - "two thousand hullahs … If there be fighting in Yaman they will have to give as loan thirty coats of mail, thirty horses and thirty camels … provided that it is not the amount of usury".
  - Also guarantees for "their churches and places of worship", and that no bishop or monk be removed.
- **al-Baladhuri**, tr. Hitti, *Origins of the Islamic State* pp. 100–101 (https://archive.org/details/originsofislamic01albauoft): "two thousand robes … thirty coats of mail, thirty mares and thirty camels … whosoever of them has up till now received usury, I am clear of the responsibility of his protection."
- None of these four gives the long covenant's distinctive clauses (per-head dirham tax, Muslim help in building churches, protection of Christian wives' faith).

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.**

Nuance for fair wording. The short treaty is tribute-centred, but it also guarantees persons, religion, churches and the clergy's offices. "Short treaty with tribute and religious guarantees" is more exact than "tribute treaty" alone.

## B13. Moritz 1918

**Finding as reported.**
- Moritz (1918) dates the Sinai text to about 900–1000 and calls it a forgery.
- He also records that Fatimid and Mamluk decrees refer to "Schutzschreiben des Propheten".

**What the primary source says.** Moritz, *Beiträge zur Geschichte des Sinaiklosters im Mittelalter nach arabischen Quellen* (1918) (https://archive.org/details/beitrgezurgesc00moriuoft; djvu text).
- **Dating:** "Die Sinaiurkunde kann also nicht vor rund 900 n. Chr. entstanden sein … und können mit ziemlicher Wahrscheinlichkeit die Herstellung auf den Raum von 900—1000 begrenzen" (the Sinai document cannot have been produced before about 900 CE … and its production can, with fair probability, be limited to the period 900–1000). He suggests the time of the caliph al-Hakim.
- **Forgery:**
  - "Daß die Fälschung besonders geschickt ausgeführt sei, läßt sich nicht behaupten" (one cannot claim that the forgery was particularly skilfully done).
  - Style: "ganz unmuhammedanisch, bisweilen kaum noch arabisch" (entirely un-Muhammadan, at times hardly Arabic at all).
- **What the decrees say:**
  - The oldest firman, of the **Fatimid** caliph al-Hafiz (1132–1149), orders the governor to keep "die alten Verträge" (the old treaties). Moritz infers that only this document can be meant.
  - The **Mamluk** sultan Qansuh al-Ghuri's firman "vom 6. Muharram 911 (921?)" names "Schutzschreiben des Propheten und Urkunden der Chalifen" (letters of protection of the Prophet and documents of the caliphs).
  - Khair Bek (the first Ottoman governor, 926/927 AH) has "das heilige prophetische Schutzschreiben" (the holy prophetic letter of protection).

**Audit verdict: Partly confirmed.**
- Confirmed: the 900–1000 dating, the forgery verdict, and the Mamluk decree's reference to the Prophet's letter of protection.
- Inexact: "Fatimid … decrees refer to 'Schutzschreiben des Propheten'". The Fatimid decree Moritz cites speaks only of "die alten Verträge" ("the old treaties"). The explicit reference to the Prophet appears in Mamluk and early Ottoman decrees.
- Suggested wording: "the earliest (Fatimid) decree refers to 'old treaties'; Mamluk and early Ottoman decrees refer explicitly to the Prophet's letter of protection."
