## A1. The "earliest Fatimid decree of 975"

**Finding as reported.** The book (pp. 71, 73) dates the earliest Fatimid decree confirming the Sinai monks' privileges to 975. Specialist sources put the earliest Sinai decree at 524/1130: Stern; Heidemann, Müller & Rāġib 1997; Atiya 1950. Moritz (1918) puts it at 1134/35.

**What the book says.**
- p. 71: "These decrees were issued by the Fatimids as early as 975,1109,1110,1135,1154, and 1156 CE (26)" (citing Atiya).
- p. 71: "While the earliest of these edicts were passed by Fatimid Shi'ite jurists in 975, the latest was issued by Ottoman Sunni scholars in 1888. If the original charter was conveyed by Sultan Selim I to Istanbul after the Ottoman invasion of Egypt in 1517, Mus­lims scholars had already had access to it for 542 years."
- p. 73: "the famous Pact of Muhammad had been confirmed by Muslims leaders and religious authorities as early as 975 CE."

**What the primary sources say.**

*Heidemann, Müller & Rāġib, *Annales islamologiques* 31 (1997), p. 1.* https://www.aai.uni-hamburg.de/voror/personen/heidemann/medien/heidemann-anislam-1997-sinai.pdf
- Text: "Le Monastère de Sainte-Catherine conserve quatre-vingt-un décrets émanés des chancelleries d'Égypte et de Syrie médiévales: six remontent à la fin de l'époque fatimide (524/1130 à 553/1158)".
- n. 2: "On trouvera l'inventaire des documents dans Atiya. Mais les publications de Stern et d'Ernst ont révélé les erreurs dont il fourmille, de dates et même de dimensions."
- n. 6: the Balat (Istanbul) document "émane probablement du calife Al-Ḥāfiẓ, FD, … n° 4".

*Princeton Geniza Project (Stern's editions).*
- Stern doc. 3: "Dhū l-Qaʿda 524 Hijrī (6 October 1130–4 November 1130 CE)". https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/41382/
- Stern doc. 4 (Istanbul metochion): "3 Rajab 529 Hijrī (19 April 1135 CE)". https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/41383/

*Atiya, *The Monastery of St. Catherine in Mount Sinai* (Cairo, preface dated February 1950), p. 51.* https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.17394
- "A preliminary investigation shows that the oldest document is dated 524 A.H./1130 A.D."

*Moritz, *Beiträge zur Geschichte des Sinaiklosters im Mittelalter* (1918), pp. 3–4.* https://archive.org/details/beitrgezurgesc00moriuoft
- The letters of protection run "in kaum unterbrochener Reihenfolge vom Jahre 1134 bis in das vorige Jahrhundert".
- The oldest surviving document is "ein Firman des Fatimidenchalifen el Hafiz (526 bis 544 der Higra = 1132–1149 n. Chr.)". Moritz found it in 1910 in the monastery's branch in Balat (p. 5 n.), i.e. Stern doc. 4 (1135).
- A footnote reports, after Cheikho, that the author of an Arabic monograph had seen in 1710 a firman "vom Jahre 508 d. H." (1114/15). Moritz marks this "(?)". The OCR is poor here.

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** No source I opened attests any Sinai decree from 975. The earliest dated items are 524/1130 (Stern, Heidemann, Atiya 1950); Moritz has 1134, and an unconfirmed report of 508/1114–15. The book's citation for 975 is Atiya 1955, p. 26, which I could not open (access-restricted). The book may therefore be repeating a date error in Atiya's 1955 hand-list, which Heidemann et al. describe as full of date errors. This does not change the finding.

## A2. Witness anachronisms and the "suspicious order" argument

**Finding as reported.** The Sinai covenant is dated 3 Muharram 2 AH. Its witnesses include Abu Hurayra (Muslim c. 7 AH) and Abu al-Dardaʾ (Muslim c. Badr or later). The Sinai lists start with ʿAli and do not include Muʿawiya. The book's p. 85 "suspicious order" argument therefore concerns the 4 AH covenants.

**What the book says.**

p. 85: "The order of the witnesses is especially suspi­cious, listing Abu Bakr first, 'Umar second, 'Uthman third, 'Ali, fourth, and Muawiyyah, fifth, as this is the traditional order of the Caliphs for the Sunnis." The sentence before it reads: "As far as Josee Balagna is concerned, the manuscripts witnessed by Mu'awiyyah (602-680 CE) in the fourth year of the hijrah are anomalous; however, the manu­scripts attributed to 'Ali, which were written in the second year … are 'plausible or likely'."

The Chapter 8 Sinai lists:
- **Sinai I** (Pococke, p. 206) and **Sinai IV** (Skrobucha, p. 212) begin "Ali, the son of Abu Thaleb; Honiar [Homar], the son of Hattavi …". The names are garbled, and there is no recognisable Muʿawiya.
- **Sinai II** (Arundale, p. 208): "Ali-ebn-Taleb, Aboubek-rebu-Aby-Kohafey, Omar ebn-el-Khattai, Otman-ebn-Assan, Abou-el-Daida, Abou-Horeyrah …".
- **Sinai III** (Géramb, p. 210): "Aly ebn Taleb, Aboubekr Aly Kohafey, Omar ebn el-Khattab, Otman ebn Hassan, About el Darda, Abou Horeyrah …".
- **Sinai V** (Haddad 1902, p. 214): "Ali Ibn Abi Talib; Abou Bekr Ibn Kahafat; Omar Ibn El-Khattab; Ottman Ibn Affan; About Darda; Abou Harirat …".
- **Sinai VI** (Morrow, p. 216): "Ali ibn Abi Talib; Abu Bakr ibn Abi Quhafah; 'Umar ibn al-Khattab; 'Uthman ibn Afjdn [Affan]; Abu al-Darda'; Abi Hurayrah …".

No Muʿawiya appears in any of the Chapter 8 texts, pp. 205–217.

The order Abu Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthman, ʿAli, Muʿawiya is that of the 1630 / 4 AH text. Book World III, p. 236: "Abu Bakr al-Siddiq; 'Umar ibn al-Khattab; 'Uthman ibn Affan; Ali ibn Abi Talib; Mu'awiyyah ibn Abi Sufyan; Abu al-Darda' …".

**What the primary sources say.**

*1630 Latin, p. 16 (SLUB image 00000022).* "Abu-Bacri assadicq. Omar ben-alchatab. Othman ben-Afan. Ali ben-abi-taleb. Moauia ben-abi-Sofian. Abu-addarda. …" https://digital.slub-dresden.de/data/kitodo/testetpai_308498003/testetpai_308498003_tif/jpegs/00000022.tif.large.jpg

*TDV "Ebû Hüreyre" (M. Y. Kandemir).* https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/ebu-hureyre
- "Ebû Hüreyre'nin 7. (628) yılın başlarında Tufeyl b. Amr ed-Devsî vasıtasıyla müslüman olduğu ve … aynı yılın muharrem ayında (Mayıs 628) Medine'ye gittiği bilinmekle beraber onun daha önce müslüman olmayıp Medine'ye İslâmiyet'i kabul etmek üzere geldiği de rivayet edilmektedir."
- In English: he became Muslim early in year 7 through Ṭufayl. One report says he came to Medina in order to accept Islam.

*TDV "Ebü'd-Derdâ" (A. Aydınlı).* https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/ebud-derda
- "Bedir Gazvesi sırasında ve ensardan en son kişi olarak müslüman olduğu rivayet edilmektedir" ("reported to have become Muslim at the time of Badr, as the last of the Ansar").

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** Every Sinai list printed in Chapter 8 starts with ʿAli and lacks Muʿawiya. Sinai II, III, V and VI include Abu Hurayra and Abu al-Dardaʾ, whose conversions TDV dates to 7 AH and to the time of Badr (Ramadan 2 AH), both after Muharram 2 AH. The "Abu Bakr … Muʿawiya fifth" order matches the 1630 (4 AH) text exactly.

Context note: in the book the sentence immediately follows Balagna's remark about the 4 AH manuscripts witnessed by Muʿawiya. A reader could therefore take it as referring to those manuscripts. It sits, however, inside the Sinai chapter's argument that the Sinai witness list was added later (p. 86: "the list of witnesses may have been attached at a later period").

## A3. Cantemir: which Mustafa Pasha, and how Cantemir frames the purchase

**Finding as reported.**
- Cantemir (*History of the Othman Empire*, Tindal tr., 1734, pp. 168–169, n. 50) names "Kiuprili ogli Mustafa Pasha", i.e. Köprülüzâde Fâzıl Mustafa, grand vizier 1689–91.
- The book (p. 72) names Merzifonlu Kara Mustafa, serving Süleyman II 1663–1666.
- Cantemir introduces the 4,000-crown purchase with "They say, though fabulously" and calls the document Mahomet's "true or forged" instrument.

**What the book says.**
- p. 72 quotes Cantemir with the name elided: "… when the Turks began to collect the Haraj upon them, and that by means of...Mustafa Pasha, who made no scruple to charge the whole Diploma with forgery … (169)".
- p. 72 then says: "As Cantemir makes manifest, the Grand Vizier Merzifonlu Kara Mustapha Pasha, who served Sulayman II from 1663-1666, denounced the Cove­nant … as a forgery as a pretext to subject Greek Chris­tians to the kharaj".
- pp. 10–11 quote Cantemir in full, including "They say, though fabulously, that Mahomet …" and "Mahomet's true or forged instrument, which the Emperor pur­chased of the monks for four thousand gold crowns".
- p. 11 concludes: "Rather than being seized, it was purchased by Selim I … would not have spent such a spectacular sum on a spuri­ous document." p. 10 calls this version "the oldest of all".

**What the primary sources say.**

*Cantemir, *The History of the Growth and Decay of the Othman Empire*, tr. N. Tindal, Part I (London: Knapton, "M DCC XXXIV" on the title page), Book III, annotation 50.* https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.02708 (part 4, leaves 43–44)
- p. 168: "since I dont think it mention'd any where but in a fabulous treatise of a Sinaitish Monk, I shall here insert. They say, though fabulously, that Mahomet being of mean birth … Long after, when Sultan Selim was in Egypt, the Abbot of Mount Sinai humbly came to him with Mahomet's true or forged Instrument, which the Emperor purchased of the Monks for four thousand Gold Crowns".
- p. 169 (page image viewed): "'Tis certain the exemption of these Monks from Tribute remain'd in force through all the Turkish dominions till the time of Soliman II. Great-Uncle of the present Emperor, when the Turks began to collect the Haraj upon them, and that by means of *Kioprili ogli Mustafa Pasha*, who made no scruple to charge the whole Diploma with forgery."
- Cantemir's own numbering calls Süleyman the Magnificent "Soliman I" (running head "Book III. 10. Soliman I."), so his "Soliman II" is the sultan who reigned 1687–91.

*TDV "Köprülüzâde Fâzıl Mustafa Paşa" (A. Özcan).* https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/kopruluzade-fazil-mustafa-pasa
- Appointed grand vizier "11 Muharrem 1101 / 25 Ekim 1689"; took the seal at Edirne on 8 November.
- Reformed the cizye by a ferman of "Cemâziyelevvel 1102 (Şubat 1691)".
- Died 1102/1691.

*TDV "Süleyman II".* "Osmanlı padişahı (1687-1691)". https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/suleyman-ii

*TDV "Merzifonlu Kara Mustafa Paşa".* https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/merzifonlu-kara-mustafa-pasa
- Died 1095/1683 (executed 25 December 1683).
- Was kapudan pasha 1661–1666 under Grand Vizier Fâzıl Ahmed and deputy grand vizier (kaymakam), i.e. under Mehmed IV.

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.**
- Minor correction to the finding: Cantemir's spelling is "Kioprili ogli Mustafa Pasha" (page image), not "Kiuprili".
- Kara Mustafa died four years before Süleyman II's accession. The book's "1663-1666" matches his posts under Mehmed IV, not under Süleyman II.
- On framing: the words "fabulously" and "true or forged" are accurately reported. The book itself quotes both phrases (pp. 10–11), so it does not hide them. What it does is draw an authenticity argument from the price.

## A4. "Griffith 63–64" is the Met catalogue entry by Hieromonk Justin

**Finding as reported.** What the book cites as "Griffith 63–64" (pp. 65, 97–98) is entry no. 37 in the Metropolitan Museum's 2012 *Byzantium and Islam* catalogue, written by Hieromonk Justin of Sinai.

**What the book says.**
- p. 65: "According to Griffith, 'the monks of Mount Sinai presented him with the original Achtiname, bearing it into his presence on a tray, covered with green brocade …' (64)".
- p. 97: "Sidney H. Griffith, however, has translated the word as 'parchment' (63)".
- p. 98: "(Griffith 63-64) … '[t]he Testament was confirmed by Sultan Selim, and by subsequent rulers, to the time of Sultan Abdul Hamid II in 1904' (64)".
- Bibliography: "Griffith, Sidney H. 'Arab Christians.' *Byzantium and Islam* … 2012. 60-62".

**What the primary source says.** Evans & Ratliff (eds.), *Byzantium and Islam: Age of Transition* (Met, 2012), archive.org scan: https://archive.org/details/2012-byzantium-and-islam.-age-of-transition. The page images are only 509×660 px, but the initials are legible.
- **Contributors list, p. xvi** (leaf n16; V2a said p. xvii):
  - "HJS Hieromonk Justin of Sinai, Librarian, Holy Monastery of Saint Catherine, Sinai, Egypt"
  - "SHG Sidney H. Griffith, Professor, Department of Semitics, The Catholic University of America"
- **p. 60:** the essay "Arab Christians, Sidney H. Griffith" begins, and catalogue entry 32 starts in the right-hand column. The catalogue entries I could read on pp. 60 and 62 end "SHG". I did not view p. 61.
- **p. 63:** entry 36 ends "SHG". Entry "37. *Achtiname* (Testament) of Muhammad" begins and says the document "was written on parchment from Ṭāʾif by the hand of Ali ibn Abi Talib".
- **p. 64:** entry 37 continues: "In 1517 … the monks of Sinai presented him with the original *Achtiname*, bearing it into his presence on a tray covered with green brocade … confirmed by Sultan Selim, and by subsequent rulers, to the time of Sultan Abdul Hamid II in 1904". It ends "… of the revelations of God. HJS".

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** The quoted content is accurate. It comes from Hieromonk Justin's entry 37, not from Griffith; Griffith's essay is the one at pp. 60–62 that the bibliography lists.

## A5. Napoleon's protection order for the monks

**Finding as reported.** Napoleon's protection order is *Correspondance de Napoléon Ier*, vol. 5, no. 3782, dated 29 frimaire an VII (19 Dec 1798). It does not mention the Prophet or the Achtiname. The book (p. 70 and elsewhere) gives 8 December and/or says Napoleon endorsed the covenant.

**What the book says.** p. 70:
- "Rather than reject the Covenant, Napo­leon … endorsed its authenticity. As Tomadakis relates, '… he also issued a special decree on 8 Decem­ber, 1798 by which the privileges of Sinai were con­firmed …' (16)".
- "In fact, the decree issued by Napoleon on 19 December 1798, in which he placed the monastery under his protection, actually confirms the privi­leges that were granted by the Prophet."

**What the primary source says.** *Correspondance de Napoléon Ier*, t. 5 (Paris: Plon, MDCCCLX), no. 3782 "ORDRE", pp. 219–220.
- Page image viewed: "Quartier général, au Caire, 29 frimaire an VII (19 décembre 1798)". The OCR text of the `napouoft` scan misreads this as "10 décembre"; the `naporich` scan and the image both read 19.
- Preamble: "pour qu'il transmette aux races futures la tradition de notre conquête; … Par respect pour Moïse et la nation juive …; Parce que le couvent du mont Sinaï est habité par des hommes instruits et policés".
- Art. 3: "Ils ne seront tenus de payer aucun droit ni tribut annuel, comme ils en ont été exemptés suivant les différents titres qu'ils en conservent."
- Muhammad, the Prophet, the Achtiname and any covenant are not named anywhere in the preamble or Arts. 1–8.
- URLs: https://archive.org/details/correspondancede05napouoft (leaf n228) ; https://archive.org/details/correspondancede05naporich

**Audit verdict: Partly confirmed.**
- **Confirmed:** the document number, date, place and content.
- **Confirmed:** the book's claims that Napoleon "endorsed its authenticity" and that the decree "confirms the privi­leges … granted by the Prophet" are not supported by the decree's text, which refers only to "les différents titres qu'ils en conservent".
- **Not as stated:** the book's own date is the correct 19 December 1798. "8 December, 1798" appears only inside the quotation from Tomadakis. The book prints it without correction, which leaves two dates on one page, but it does not adopt it.

## A6. Thenaud: "compte et reliquat"

**Finding as reported.** Thenaud (*Le voyage d'outremer*, ed. Schefer 1884, p. 72) says the prior "se rendit mammelu quant on lui demanda compte et reliquat de son administration". The book (p. 19) renders this as "when asked to assume its administration".

**What the book says.** p. 19:
- "In front of that church is a Mus­lim mosque which was built by prior [a prieur de leans] who became a Muslim when asked to assume its administration' (72)."
- It continues: "the Fatimids, who were the rulers at the time, granted him the administration of the mosque on their behalf … the Covenant … was in Muslims hands from the moment the mosque was built in the 12th century CE until it fell into disuse in the 14th century".

**What the primary source says.** Thenaud, *Le voyage d'outremer*, ed. Ch. Schefer (Paris: Leroux, 1884), p. 72. https://archive.org/details/levoyagedoutreme00then

"Devant ceste église est une musquete de Mores, que fit bastir ung prieur de leans qui se rendit mammelu quant on lui demanda compte et reliquat de son administration, en laquelle sont les privilèges que Mahommet donna es caloyers, c'est assavoir immunité de tous tributz et devoirs; et sont signez de sa main, plongée en encre et assise sur ledict papier".

"Demander compte et reliquat" is accounting language: to demand a statement of account and the outstanding balance of a stewardship.

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** The French says the prior turned Mamluk (renegade) when called to account for his administration. It does not say he was asked to assume the administration of the mosque. Thenaud gives no date and does not mention the Fatimids. The book's inferences about Fatimid appointment and Muslim custody from the 12th to the 14th century have no basis in this passage.

## A7. Burckhardt and the AH 783 record

**Finding as reported.** Burckhardt (*Travels in Syria and the Holy Land*, 1822, pp. 543–544) used the AH 783 record to show that the mosque existed long before Selim. The book (p. 18) says Burckhardt "interprets this as proof that the mosque dates to 1381 CE".

**What the book says.** p. 18 quotes the AH 783 passage, via Manley and 'Abdel-Hakim 144, then says: "While Burckhardt interprets this as proof that the mosque dates to 1381 CE, to me it implies that the mosque already existed and that the monks merely hired some stranded Muslims to act as its guardians and custodians."

**What the primary source says.** Burckhardt, *Travels in Syria and the Holy Land* (London: Murray, 1822), pp. 543–544. https://archive.org/details/b22017355
- The monks' tradition is that the mosque "was built in the sixteenth century", after Selim's conquest.
- "This tradition, however, is contradicted by some old Arabic records kept by the prior, in which I read a circumstantial account how, in the year of the Hedjra 783, some straggling Turkish Hadjis … were retained here, and a salary settled on them and their descendants, on condition of their becoming the servants of the mosque. The conquest of Egypt by Selim did not take place till A. H. 895. The mosque in the convent of Sinai appears therefore to have existed long before the time of Selim."

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** Burckhardt does not date the mosque to 1381. He uses the AH 783 record to show that the mosque already existed then, which is the inference the book presents as its own.

Side notes:
- Burckhardt's own "A. H. 895" for Selim's conquest is itself wrong (the conquest was 922–923 AH), but that does not affect his argument.
- The book quotes Burckhardt second-hand, so the misreading may originate in Manley and 'Abdel-Hakim. I did not check them.

## A8. When the year "1517" first appears; Moritz's "Selim or Süleyman"

**Finding as reported.** Among the sources checked, the explicit year "1517" for Selim's removal of the original first appears in print in Arundale (1837) and Géramb (1840). Moritz (1918) had already reported a "Selim or Süleyman" tradition and favoured Süleyman.

**What the book says.**
- p. 68: "While every authority has accepted 1517 as the year in which the Covenant was taken to the capi­tal … (Ratfliff 14-15)".
- p. 69: "If the original was indeed at Saint Catherine's between 1533-1534, then it may have been taken by Sultan Sulayman the Magnificent, who ruled from 1520 to his death in 1566 (Mouton 178)."
- p. 67 attributes the 1517 removal also to Monconys (*Le voyage* 92) and Pococke (148).

**What the primary sources say.** Earlier sources name Selim but give no year:
- **Monconys** (visit 1647; IFAO ed.): "laquelle Selin Empereur des Turcs leur osta, & l'emporta dans son thresor". https://archive.org/details/voyageenegyptede00monc
- **Cantemir 1734**, p. 168: "when Sultan Selim was in Egypt".
- **Pococke 1743**, vol. 1, p. 148: "in their hands to the time of Sultan Selim, who … took it from them". https://archive.org/details/b30456307_0001
- **Burckhardt 1822**, p. 546: "remained in the convent until Selim the First conquered Egypt, when … he sent for it".

The first explicit "1517" I found:
- **Arundale 1837**: "In [1]517, after the conquest of Egypt, Selim demanded it, and placed a copy, in parchment, certified by himself, among the archives of this monastery. M. Mauchin gives it in his work on Egypt." https://archive.org/details/illustrationsofj00arun
- **Géramb 1840**, vol. 2, pp. 245–246: "In 1517, after the conquest of Egypt, Selim I. demanded it". https://archive.org/details/vol2pilgrimageto00gera

**Moritz 1918**, p. 5 (https://archive.org/details/beitrgezurgesc00moriuoft):
- "Das Original selbst ist verschwunden; nach der Tradition soll es von Sultan Selim oder seinem Sohne, Sultan Suleiman, in das Staatsarchiv von Konstantinopel überführt worden sein."
- pp. 5–6: the surviving copies date from 957 and 968 AH, and Süleyman died only in 974 AH, "so ist es höchstwahrscheinlich, daß er es gewesen ist, der diese Urkunden aus dem Kloster hat entführen lassen."

**Audit verdict: Confirmed, with qualifications.**
- The numeral "1517" is absent from Monconys, Cantemir, Pococke and Burckhardt. Burckhardt and Cantemir do tie the removal to Selim's conquest of, or presence in, Egypt, so 1517 is implicit in them.
- Arundale names Mauchin as his source. Mauchin's work was not located, so an earlier explicit "1517" in Mauchin cannot be ruled out.
- The Moritz part is confirmed. Fairness note: the book attributes its Süleyman suggestion to Mouton (178), not to itself alone. It does not cite Moritz for it.

## A9. Topkapı: letters of the Prophet in the Sacred Relics, and the Achtiname

**Finding as reported.** What the official Millî Saraylar / Topkapı pages and the TDV encyclopedia say about letters of the Prophet in the Sacred Relics collection: V2a reports four, to al-Muqawqis, al-Mundhir b. Sāwā, Musaylima and al-Ḥārith. Also, whether any official source says the Sinai Achtiname is there.

**What the book says.**
- p. 69: the Chamber of Relics "is devoted to the holy artifacts that Selim I brought back from his conquest of Egypt in 1517. The Chamber of Relics contains the Prophet's mantle, his swords, and the letter he wrote to Muqawqis".
- p. 69: "The possibility that the original Achtiname remains stored in some forgotten archive, perhaps in the Palace of Topkapi in Istanbul, Turkey, is tan­talizing to say the least."

**What the primary sources say.**

## A10. BnF MS Arabe 214: provenance, and Vansleb's travel dates

**Finding as reported.** The BnF record of MS Arabe 214 gives the provenance "Acheté par Vansleb en Orient", with Vansleb seals, a reading mark dated 1673, and a reader "Ġabriyāl Ṣahyūn al-Ziftāwī". The book (pp. 144–146) says "there is no question" that this is the manuscript Scaliger brought and Sionita printed. The finding also checks Vansleb's travel dates against the book's "1671 and 1672".

**What the book says.**
- p. 144–145 reproduces the BnF "History of Conservation" ("Purchased by Vansleb in the Orient. Seals with the numbers of Vansleb … reading mark belonging to Butrus ibn Diyab al-Halabi, dated 1673 CE …"). It prints "9th of Hatur 1380" where the BnF has 1308.
- p. 145: "[Vansleb] made his two trips in 1671 and in 1672. Since the Testa­ment of the Prophet was published in 1630 by Gab­riel Sionita, Vansleb could not possibly have purchased it in Egypt forty years later. Further­more, the manuscript contains the mark of Gabriyal Sahyun al-Ziftawi, namely, that of Gab­riel Sionita himself! … there is no question that this is the actual document that he brought back from the Middle East."

**What the primary sources say.**

*BnF Archives et manuscrits, Arabe 214.* https://archivesetmanuscrits.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cc894877
- "Historique de la conservation: Une notice de Renaudot. Acheté par Vansleb en Orient. Cachets au chiffre de Vansleb."
- "marque de lecture de Buṭrus ibn Diyāb al-Ḥalabī, datée de 1673 J.-C., et de Manṣūr ibn Sulaymān Ṣahyūn al-Ramādī, datée du 14 Babā 1336 des Martyrs [1620 J.-C.] (f. 2); mention de l'arrivée d'un missionnaire au Vieux-Caire, le 9 Hatūr 1308 des Martyrs (1592 J.-C.) et marque de Ġabriyāl Ṣahyūn al-Ziftāwī (f. 2 v)".
- Old shelfmarks: "385 (Regius)", "Ancien fonds arabe 88". Item 12: "F. 257-261 • Charte accordée à tous les chrétiens".

*Vansleb, *Nouvelle relation en forme de journal, d'un voyage fait en Egypte … en 1672 & 1673* (Paris 1677), pp. 1–4.* https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_8n2JTTnOoMIC
- He was at Marseille on 8 May 1671 and sailed on the 20th. He was at Aleppo from September to December 1671, then Damascus and Sidon.
- He embarked at Sidon on 19 February (the text prints "1671"; the margin reads 1672) and reached the Damietta mouth of the Nile on the 18th of the following month. He observed Egypt "pendant vingt mois".

*Pougin, *Vansleb, savant orientaliste et voyageur* (1869).* https://archive.org/details/vanslebsavantor00pouggoog
- p. 16: "Vansleb partit le 15 Juin 1663, et arriva au Caire en Janvier 1664. Il employa une année à visiter l'Égypte".
- He left Egypt in mid-October 1673.
- He sent manuscripts to Paris "dans les années 1671, 1672 et 1673".

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** The BnF record reads exactly as reported, and the book quotes it while arguing against it. Vansleb's journeys were 1663–64 (Cairo from January 1664, about a year) and 1671–73 (Egypt from March 1672 to about October 1673). The book's "two trips in 1671 and in 1672" is wrong, and its rebuttal assumes the identification it sets out to prove.

Corrections and caveats:
- **V4's duration.** V4's "about 12 months in Egypt (1672–73)" understates the second stay, which was about 19–20 months.
- **The 1673 reader.** Wikidata Q15840509, "Pierre Dipy", has the Arabic label بطرس دياب الحلبي (Butrus Diyab al-Halabi). German Wikipedia, used only as a lead, describes him as an Aleppine Maronite in Paris who catalogued the Bibliothèque du Roi's oriental manuscripts. If he is the reader, the 1673 mark could have been made in Paris. It would then not, on its own, show that the manuscript was still in the East in 1673. The Vansleb purchase note and seals are unaffected.
- **"al-Ziftāwī".** The BnF does not identify him. I did not independently verify the Zifta (Egypt) derivation of the nisba.

## A11. The date in the 1630 Paris edition

**Finding as reported.** The 1630 Paris edition (SLUB Dresden digitised copy) dates the covenant "Monday (*yawm al-ithnayn*), at the completion of four months of the fourth year". The Latin reads "die Lunae ultima mensis quarti". No month is named. The book (pp. 147, 236) has "the second day of the month of Rabi' Ashar".

**What the book says.**
- p. 147: "the Covenant of the Prophet Muhammad with the Christians of the World dates from the second day of the month of Rabi' Ashar of the fourth year after the hijrah."
- World III ("[1630]", p. 236): "Written [down] by Mu'awiyyah ibn Abi Sufyan, and dictated by the Messenger of Allah on the second day of the month of Rabi' Ashar during the fourth year of the Hegira in Medina."

**What the primary source says.** *Testamentum et pactiones initae inter Mohamedem et Christianae fidei cultores* (Paris: Vitray, 1630), SLUB Dresden Hist.Turc.524 (page images viewed).
- **Arabic, pp. 15–16** (images 00000037, 00000038): "وكتب معاوية بن ابي سفيان من املا رسول الله يوم الاثنين تمام اربعة اشهر من السنة / الرابعة من الهجره بالمدينه". That is: "Muʿāwiya b. Abī Sufyān wrote [it] at the dictation of the Messenger of God, on Monday, at the completion of four months of the fourth year of the Hijra, in Medina."
- **Latin, p. 16** (image 00000022): "Secretarius autem fuit Moauia ben-abi-Sofian, miles Nuntij Dei, die Lunae vltima mensis quarti, anni quarti Hegirae, in Medina."
- URLs: https://digital.slub-dresden.de/data/kitodo/testetpai_308498003/testetpai_308498003_tif/jpegs/00000037.tif.large.jpg ; …/00000038.tif.large.jpg ; …/00000022.tif.large.jpg

**Audit verdict: Confirmed.** Neither the Arabic nor the Latin names a month, and neither has "second day". "Rabiʿ Ashar" is not a standard month name; the fourth month is Rabiʿ al-Akhir. That "second day" renders *yawm al-ithnayn* (literally "day two", i.e. Monday) is a plausible explanation, but it is an inference.

## A12. "Dr. Addison"

**Finding as reported.** The book (pp. 153–154, 157) identifies "Dr. Addison" as James Thayer Addison (1887–1953), a defender. The cited source (*A General Dictionary, Historical and Critical*, vol. 1, 1735, art. "ADDISON (LANCELOT)") concerns Lancelot Addison (d. 1703). That article reports an Arabist's view that Scaliger probably forged the text.

**What the book says.**
- p. 153: "The Covenant … is also treated as factual by Henri Layard …, Pedro de Madrazo …, and the Société d'Amis …, as well as James Thayer Addison (1887-1953 CE)."
- pp. 153–154: "A detailed defense of the covenant was also made by Dr. Addison whose arguments were sum­marized by Bayle as follows: Dr Addison ... mentions the treaty of Alliance … Dr. Addison says, that we may consider it as one of Mahomet's artifices to support his authority … (241)".
- p. 154: "The views of Dr. Addison and Van Dyke are evi­dently incorrect."
- p. 157: "When Addison asked a person who was very skilled in the Arabic language … The reader concluded that it was very probable that the covenant had been forged by the missionary in question (Bayle 241)."
- The bibliography lists Bayle et al., *A General Dictionary*, vol. 1 (1735). It has no Addison entry. Lancelot Addison is not named anywhere in the book.

**What the primary source says.** *A General Dictionary, Historical and Critical*, vol. 1 (London; the archive.org copy's title page reads "MDCCXXXIV"), art. "ADDISON (LANCELOT)", p. 241. https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_a-general-dictionary-hi_bayle-pierre_1741_1
- The subject: "Lancelot Addison, a Minister, born at Mauldismeaburne … installed [Dean of Lichfield] June 19th 1683 … He died in 1703".
- On the treaty, in *The First State of Mahumedism*: "Dr. Addison says, that we may consider it as one of Mahomet's artifices to support his authority, which was then in its infancy; but when he afterwards saw it sufficiently established, he changed his language …".
- On the Arabist: "Dr. Addison tells us afterwards, that having asked a person very well skill'd in the Arabic tongue, what he thought of this treaty …, he replied, that when he had read it, he was of opinion it was a spurious piece, because he found nothing in the stile, which was like that of the Koran; that … this piece was brought from the East by a Capuchin, named Pacifique Scaliger; and that it is very probable it had been forged by that Missionary."

**Audit verdict: Partly confirmed.**
- **Confirmed:** the cited article concerns Lancelot Addison (d. 1703) and reports the Arabist's forgery opinion. A 1730s dictionary cannot be summarising James Thayer Addison (b. 1887).
- **Confirmed:** the quoted passage is not a defence of authenticity. Addison treats the treaty as a temporary "artifice" of Muhammad, and the book itself calls his views "evidently incorrect" (p. 154).
- **Not quite as stated:** the book never explicitly says that "Dr. Addison" is James Thayer Addison. It names J. T. Addison as treating the covenant as factual, then credits "Dr. Addison" with a "detailed defense" in the next sentences. It identifies no other Addison. The conflation is implied by juxtaposition, not stated outright.
- Minor: the copy I opened is dated 1734 on its title page, not 1735.

## A13. Printing history, and the 1795 cancellation

**Finding as reported.** The book (p. 151) says the 1630 edition was reprinted four times in its first year, and again in 1638, 1655, 1668, 1690 and 1888. Library catalogues (VD17 etc.) show 1638 (Fabricius, Latin only), 1655 (Nissel) and 1690 (Hinckelmann), but no 1668 or 1888 edition. The 1795 French committee formally cancelled its printing order on 3 brumaire IV (25 Oct 1795).

**What the book says.**
- p. 151: "Since the work sold out almost immediately, and book­stores could not meet the demand, the work was re-published four times during the first year of publication. It was republished in various editions and languages by other translators and publishers in 1655,1638, 1668,1690, and as late as 1888 CE."
- p. 155: "Mr. Lefebvre-Villebrune was suddenly removed from the project and replaced by a certain Mr. Legrand (Guillaume 858) … To the best of my knowl­edge, the translation, publication, and dissemina­tion … never saw the light of day."

- **Caveats:**

  - The catalogues also show printings the book omits: 1645, 1660/61 and 1683/84.
