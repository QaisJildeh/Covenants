## 2. Each text version in detail

### Ch. 8 — Covenant with the Monks of Mount Sinai

#### Sinai I — Pococke (1809), quoted from Davenport — pp. 205–206 [PDF 230–231]
- **Issuer/opening:** "Muhammad the son of 'Abd Allah, the Messenger of Allah, and careful guardian of the whole world; has wrote the present instrument…" (p. 205).
- **Recipients:** "all those who are in his national people, and of his own religion, as a secure and positive promise to be accomplished to the Christian nation and relations of the Nazarene, whosoever they may be, whether they be the noble or the vulgar, the honorable or otherwise" (p. 205). The provisions concern "the monks" (I–XI) and "every Christian" (XII–XVIII).
- **Date:** "in the second year of the Hegira, the third day of the month of Muharram" (p. 206). No CE date.
- **Place:** "at the mosque of the Prophet" (p. 206).
- **Scribe:** "This present was written by the leader, the successor, 'Ali the son of Abu Talib; the Prophet marking it with his hand at the mosque of the Prophet (in whom be peace)" (p. 206).
- **Witnesses (16).** Heading "Witnesses," (p. 206). OCR spellings:
  1. Ali, the son of Abu Thaleb
  2. Honiar [OCR; likely Homar, as in IV], the son of Hattavi
  3. Ziphir, the son of Abuan
  4. Saith, the son of Maat
  5. Thavitt, the son of Nesis
  6. Atnphachin [OCR; likely Amphachin], the son of Hassan
  7. Muathem, the son of Kasvi
  8. Azur, the son of jassin [Jassin]
  9. Abombaker, the son of Anibi [OCR; possibly Ambi]
  10. Kaphe Ottoman, the son of Gafas
  11. Ambtelack, the son of Messutt
  12. Phazer, the son of Abbas
  13. Talat, the son of Atnptolack [OCR; likely Amptolack]
  14. Saat the son of Abbatt
  15. Kastner the son of Abid
  16. Atnbtullack [OCR; likely Ambtullack] the son of Omar
- **Provisions:** 18 numbered "chapters" (I–XVIII). A bracketed note after XI reads: "[In these eleven chapters is to be found whatever relates to the monks, as to the remaining seven chapters, they direct what relates to every Christian.]" (p. 206).
  1. (I) Any of "my nation" who breaks the promise and oath destroys the promise of God and is "worthy of the curse, whether he be the King himself, or a poor man".
  2. (II) Wherever monks settle (mountain, hill, village, habitable place, sea, desert, convent, church, house of prayer), the Prophet is "the preserver and protector of them, their goods and effects", jointly with all his people, "because they are a part of my own people, and an honor to me."
  3. (III) Officers are not to require any poll-tax or other tribute from them.
  4. (IV) No one may change their judges or governors. These remain in office "without being deported".
  5. (V) No one may molest them when they travel on the road.
  6. (VI) No one may deprive them of their churches.
  7. (VII) Whoever annuls these decrees "annuls the ordinance of God".
  8. (VIII) Their judges, governors, monks, servants, disciples and dependents pay no poll-tax and are not molested. The Prophet protects them by land or sea, east, west, north or south.
  9. (IX) Solitaries on the mountains: no poll-tax or tithes on their incomes, and no Muslim may partake of what they have.
  10. (X) When the crop is plentiful, the inhabitants must give them "a certain measure" out of every bushel.
  11. (XI) In wartime they are not taken from their dwellings or compelled to go to war, and no poll-tax is required of them.
  12. (XII) Christians who are inhabitants and able to pay the poll-tax "shall pay no more than twelve drachms."
  13. (XIII) Nothing else may be required of them. Quotes God: "'Do not molest those that have a veneration for the books that are sent from God, but rather in a kind manner give of your good things to them, and converse with them, and hinder everyone from molesting them' [29:46]".
  14. (XIV) If a Christian woman marries a Muslim man, he shall not keep her "from her church and prayers, and the practice of her religion."
  15. (XV) "That no person hinder them from repairing their churches." This is a ban on hindrance; it does not promise help.
  16. (XVI) Whoever acts contrary "becomes truly an apostate to God, and to his divine apostle".
  17. (XVII) No one shall bear arms against them, and "the Muslims shall wage war for them."
  18. (XVIII) No one of "my nation" shall act contrary "until the end of the world."
- Stray OCR on p. 206: "1" and "-28" between XIV and XV. These may be marginalia or a page artifact.

#### Sinai II — Arundale (1837), from Mauchin's French — pp. 207–208 [PDF 232–233]
- **Issuer:** "Mohammed-ebn-Abdallah, gives this Edict for all the world in general… the confidant of God, and charged with the keeping of all created beings."
- **Recipients:** "for my nation, and for all those who are Christians; from the east and from the west, far and near; for all those that are eloquent and not eloquent, the learned and the ignorant." Violators are cursed "whoever they are, Sultan or other Musulman."
- **Date:** "the 3 Moharran [sic/OCR], the second year of the Hegira, and of J.C. the 1st of August, 622" (p. 208).
- **Place:** Not stated.
- **Scribe:** "This Edict was written by the hand of Aby-Taleb". The name is given as Aby-Taleb only. "It is signed by the Prophet himself. Happy he who confirms." (p. 208).
- **Witnesses (22, counted by commas).** Heading: "The testimonies which attest the truth of this Edict… are" (p. 208).
  1. Ali-ebn-Taleb
  2. Aboubek-rebu-Aby-Kohafey
  3. Omar ebn-el-Khattai
  4. Otman-ebn-Assan
  5. Abou-el-Daida
  6. Abou-Horeyrah
  7. AbdallahAbou-Massaoud
  8. Abbat-ebn-Abdel-Motbb
  9. Fodeyl-ebn-Abbas
  10. Zobeir-ebn-Aouan
  11. Talhat-ebn-Obeydallah
  12. Saad-ebn-Maoz
  13. Saad-ebn-Obadey
  14. Thabet-ebn-Keys
  15. MonKayetmeth
  16. Hachem-ebn-Ommyeh
  17. Hareth-ebn-Thabet
  18. Abdallah-ebn Atnrou [OCR; likely Amrou]
  19. Ebn-el-Ass
  20. Amer-ebn-Yassin
  21. Moazzam-ebn-Kerachy
  22. Abdel-Azini [OCR; likely Azim]-ebn-Hassan
- **Provisions:**
  1. If "a father or a hermit" retires to a mountain, grotto, plain, desert, town, village or church, the Prophet is "behind him as his protector against all enemies, myself in person, my forces and my subjects, although these priests are cancelled by me" (*sic*; III renders this as "as those priests are my rayas"). "I will avoid doing them any hurt."
  2. No contributions may be taken from them "unless voluntarily".
  3. No bishop may be changed from his bishopric, priest from his priesthood, or hermit from his hermitage.
  4. None of the objects in their churches may enter the construction of mosques or Muslim dwellings.
  5. It is forbidden to exact contributions from priests, bishops or devotees. Their prerogatives are preserved everywhere, by land and sea, in all four directions.
  6. Those who sow and plant in the mountains and scattered places pay no tithes or contributions, "not even voluntarily", when the produce is for their nourishment.
  7. If they lack corn, "every house shall assist them with a measure".
  8. They are not obliged to serve in the wars or to pay imposts.
  9. Owners of goods or merchandise pay "not more than twelve drachms of silver every year."
  10. "No one must be molested; it is forbidden to enter into discussion with them by soft measures, putting aside everything disagreeable, and preserving the wing of compassion." The wording is as printed.
  11. A Christian woman who goes to the house of a Muslim "must be well treated, and allowed to perform her prayer in a church".
  12. "The Christians shall be assisted to preserve their churches and houses".
  13. They are not obliged to carry arms. "the Musulmen shall bear them for them".
  14. The ordinance is not to be disobeyed "to the end of the world."

#### Sinai III — Géramb (1840), from Mauchin's French — pp. 209–210 [PDF 234–235]
- **Issuer:** "Mohamed ebn Abdallah has issued this edict for the whole world in general… the friend of God".
- **Recipients:** "for my nation and for all those who are in Christendom, in the East, or in the West, near and afar off, for all who are eloquent and not eloquent, known and unknown". Violators: "sultan or any other Musulman."
- **Date:** "the 3rd of Mohanam [OCR; Moharram], in the second year of the Hegira, and of Jesus Christ, the 1st of August, 622" (p. 210).
- **Place:** Not stated.
- **Scribe:** "This edict was written by the hand of Aly ebn Taleb… it is signed by the Prophet himself. Happy he who shall do, and woe to him who shall not do according to its contents." (p. 210).
- **Witnesses (21).** Heading: "The witnesses who attest the truth of this edict… are:" (p. 210).
  1. Aly ebn Taleb
  2. Aboubekr Aly Kohafey
  3. Omar ebn el-Khattab
  4. Otman ebn Hassan
  5. About el Darda
  6. Abou Horeyrah
  7. Abdallah Abou Massaoud
  8. Abbas ebn Abdel Motteb
  9. Fodeyl ebn Abbas
  10. Zobeir ebn Aouan
  11. Talhat ebn Obeydallah
  12. Saad ebn Maoz
  13. Saad ebn Obadey
  14. Thabet ebn Kays Mou Khayetmeth (one entry here; II splits it into two)
  15. Hachem ebn Omyeh
  16. Hareth ebn Thabet
  17. Abdallah ebn Arnrou [OCR; Amrou]
  18. Ebn el Ass
  19. Amer ebn Yassin
  20. Meazzam ebn Kerachy
  21. Adel Azirn [OCR; Azim] eben Hassan
- **Provisions.** Same order as II, with these wording differences:
  1. A priest or hermit is protected "as those priests are my rayas [Christian subjects]".
  2. "Voluntary contributions only shall be taken from them, and they shall not be constrained to pay any."
  3. No bishop may be driven from his bishopric, "nor a priest from his religion", nor a hermit from his hermitage.
  4. Nothing belonging to their churches may be used to build mosques or Muslim dwellings.
  5. No contributions may be imposed on priests, bishops or religious men. Their privileges and "my safeguard" hold everywhere.
  6. Those who sow or plant in mountains and sequestered places pay no tithes or contributions, not even voluntarily, when the produce is for their own subsistence.
  7. If they need corn, "they shall be assisted with one measure for each house".
  8. They are not obliged to go to war or pay any taxes.
  9. "Those who possess immoveable property or merchandise shall not give more than twelve silver drachmas per year."
  10. None shall be molested, and no one shall "enter into strife with those who follow the precepts of Gospel, but behave mildly towards them".
  11. "When a Christian woman shall join the Musulmans, they shall treat her well, and permit her to go and pray in a church".
  12. "The Christians shall be assisted to preserve their churches and their houses, which will assist them to preserve their religion".
  13. They are not obliged to bear arms, and Muslims bear arms for them.
  14. The ordinance stands "until the end of the world."

#### Sinai IV — Skrobucha, *Sinai* (1966): 58 — pp. 211–212 [PDF 236–237]
- **Issuer:** "this letter is addressed by Muhammad, son of Abdullah, he who proclaims and admonishes men…"
- **Recipients:** "people of this faith and to all in the world who profess the Christian religion in East and West, near and far, whether they are Arabs or non-Arabs, unknown or known". Violators: "whether he be a sultan or any one so ever of the faithful of Islam."
- **Date:** "on the third of Muharram in the year 2 of the Prophet's Hegira..." (p. 212). The text breaks off in an ellipsis.
- **Place:** "in the Mosque of the Prophet" (p. 212).
- **Scribe:** "This promise of protection was written in his own hand by Ali bin Abu Talib". The witness heading just before it says "this letter of protection, written by Muhammad, son of Abdullah, God's Messenger" (p. 212).
- **Witnesses (16).** The list is identical in content to Pococke's (I):
  1. Ali the son of Abu Thaleb
  2. Homar, the son of Hattavi
  3. Ziphir, the son of Abuan
  4. Saith, the son of Maat
  5. Thavitt the son of Nesis
  6. Amphachin, the son of Hassan
  7. Muathem, the son of Kasvi
  8. Azur, the son of ]assin [Jassin]
  9. Abotnbaker [OCR; Abombaker], the son of Ambi Kaphe
  10. Ottoman, the son of Gafas
  11. Ambtelaok [OCR; Ambtelack], the son of Messutt
  12. Phazer, the son of Abbas
  13. Talat, the son of Amptolack
  14. Saat the son of Abbatt
  15. Kastner the son of Abid
  16. Ambtullack the son of Omar

  Punctuation differs from I at items 9–10: I has "Abombaker, the son of Anibi; Kaphe Ottoman, the son of Gafas", while IV has "…the son of Ambi Kaphe; Ottoman, the son of Gafas".
- **Closing:** "Praise be to all you abide by its contents, and cursed be all who do not observe it." (p. 212).
- **Provisions:**
  1. If a monk or pilgrim seeks protection (mountain, valley, cave, tilled fields, plain, desert, church), "I am with him, and defend him… I, my helpers, all men of my faith, and all my followers, for these people are my followers and my protégés."
  2. They are protected "from interference with the supply of victuals".
  3. They pay no taxes "over and above what they themselves approve". No compulsion or constraint.
  4. No bishop may be removed from his bishopric, monk from his monastery or hermit from his tower, and no pilgrim may be hindered from his pilgrimage.
  5. "no church or chapel shall be destroyed".
  6. Church property may not be used to build mosques or Muslim houses.
  7. "Neither poll-tax nor impost shall be laid on monks, bishops, or hermits". They are protected everywhere.
  8. Those in the solitude of the mountains or at the holy places are free of poll-tax and of tithe or duty on what they grow for their own use.
  9. They are helped to raise a corn-crop "by a free allowance of one qadah [unit of dry measure] in every ardabb [= 6 waybah = 24 rub'a]".
  10. They are not obliged to serve in war or pay the poll-tax.
  11. Those liable to land-tax, or with resources in land or commerce, "shall not have to pay more than ten dirhams a head a year". Note: ten, not twelve.
  12. No unjust tax. "with these people of the Book there is to be no strife, unless it be over what is for the good." No Qur'an reference is given here.
  13. "We wish to take them under the wing of our mercy". Vexation is kept away from them.
  14. A Christian woman entering a Muslim household is received with kindness and given opportunity to pray in her church. "there shall be no dispute between her and a man who loves her religion."
  15. They "shall be assisted in the improvement of their churches and religious dwellings".
  16. None is compelled to bear arms, and the Muslims defend them.
  17. This holds "until the hour comes and the last day breaks upon the world."

#### Sinai V — Anton [E/F.] Haddad (1902) — pp. 213–214 [PDF 238–239]
- **Issuer:** "Mohammed, Ibn Abdullah, the Messenger, the Prophet, the Faithful".
- **Recipients:** "This letter is directed to the embracers of Islam, as a covenant given to the followers of Nazarene in the East and West, the far and near, the Arabs and foreigners, the known and the unknown." Violators: "whether he is a Sultan or any other believer of Islam."
- **Date:** "on the third day of Moharram in the second year of El-Hajrih" (p. 214).
- **Place:** "in the worship place of the Prophet" (p. 214).
- **Scribe:** "This oath was written by the hand of Ali ibn Abi Talib". The text adds "It was signed by the great Assistants of Mohammed, as follows." (p. 214).
- **Witnesses (22).**
  1. Ali Ibn Abi Talib
  2. Abou Bekr Ibn Kahafat
  3. Omar Ibn El-Khattab
  4. Ottman Ibn Affan
  5. About Darda
  6. Abou Harirat
  7. Abdullah Ibn Masood
  8. Abbas Ibn Abdoul Mottaleb
  9. El-Fadhl Ibn Abbas
  10. Ezzobier Ibn El-Awam
  11. Talhat Ibn Abdullah
  12. Said Ibn Maath
  13. Said Ibn Abada
  14. Thabit Ibn Nafees
  15. Zied Ibn Thabit
  16. Abou Hanifa Ibn Attaba
  17. Hashim Ibn Obied
  18. Maazatn [OCR; Maazam] Ibn Kariesh
  19. El-Harith Ibn Thabit
  20. Abdoul Azim Ibn Haasan
  21. Abdullah Ibn Omar Ibn El-Aas
  22. Aarnir [OCR; Aamir] Ibn Yasir
- **Provisions:**
  1. "Whenever monies [*sic*/OCR; probably 'monks'], devotees and pilgrims gather" (mountain, valley, den, frequented place, plain, church, houses of worship), "we are [at the] back of them and shall protect them, and their properties and their morals, by Myself, by My Friends and by My Assistants".
  2. They are exempt from the burdens others pay "as an oath of allegiance". "They must not give anything of their income but that which pleases them". They must not be offended, disturbed, coerced or compelled.
  3. Their judges may not be changed or kept from their offices. Monks may not be disturbed in their religious order, and "people of seclusion" may not be stopped from living in their cells.
  4. No one may plunder the pilgrims, destroy or spoil their churches or houses of worship, or take anything from them to "the houses of Islam".
  5. No poll-tax on their judges, monks and worshippers, and no fine, tax "or any unjust right".
  6. They are protected at sea and on land, in all four directions.
  7. No taxes or tithes from those who worship in the mountains "or from those who cultivate the Holy Lands". No interference or legal action against them.
  8. At harvest "they should be given a Kadah for each Ardab of wheat (about five bushels and a half)".

### Ch. 9 — Covenant with the Christians of Persia — Arpee 1946: 355–360 — pp. 223–226 [PDF 248–251]
- **Recipients:** "all Christian nations, such as dwell throughout the world toward the eastward of Arabia and Persia, or within the bounds of them, whether they be in immediate contact with the Faithful or whether they be distant". Violators: "whether he be a king, or one of the people, whether he be a pious believer (sc., a Moslem), or only a believer (sc., a Christian)." (p. 223).
- **Date:** "this Treaty was drawn up on the Monday following the first four months of the Fourth Year of the Hegira." (p. 226).
- **Place:** Not stated.
- **Scribe:** Not named.
- **Witnesses:** None named. The text says: "In witness whereof is attached the Signature that in the presence of the Clergy and the Lords of the Nation, the Holy, Great Prophet, Mohammed, affixed, confirming the foregoing Treaty." (p. 226).
- **Structure:** (a) the covenant; (b) a bracketed passage: "[Here also follows a Treaty of Mohammed… with the Christian people, a Treaty which His Majesty after the foregoing words commanded and established with the Christians…]" (p. 225); (c) commandments binding the Christians.
- **Provisions, part (a): obligations on Muslims.**
  1. Muslims shall defend and aid "believers" (Christians) far or near, and throughout Christendom protect their places of worship and the dwellings of their monks and priests, everywhere (mountains, plains, towns, waste places, deserts, West and East, sea and land), both "in their faith and in their property".
  2. Muslims shall care for them "as being under our protection" and aid them in distress, "for they are a people subject to my Nation".
  3. Taxes: "not to exact more than they are able to pay, but to adjust matters with their consent, without force or violence."
  4. "Their building enterprises shall not be interfered with". Priests are not molested. They are not persecuted for faith or customs, and may pray in their own places by their own rites.
  5. Churches shall not be dismantled or destroyed, nor homes and mansions confiscated "for mosques or residences, without their consent."
  6. "No land taxes shall be exacted from them in excess of the value of four dinars, or one linen sheet". This is to be applied for the Muslims' benefit "and held as a sacred trust for public use".
  7. Poll-tax: merchants, the wealthy, open-country dwellers, pearl-fishers, and owners of mines of precious stones, gold or silver, "shall not be made to pay more than twelve dirhams."
  8. "Of those who are not of the Christian faith [and] neither conduct worship according to the Christian rite, four dirhams shall be exacted." Those who conform to that people pay no more than "the aforementioned Twelve Dirhams, provided that they dwell where all their people are resident."
  9. Travellers without permanent abode pay no land tax. The exception is an heir to property "on which the Imam has a legal claim", and even then without violence or excess.
  10. Christians are not asked to fight for Muslims, or forced to join Muslim wars. If an enemy attacks the Christians, Muslims shall defend them with horses, swords and spears.
  11. "No Christians shall be brought by force to confess Islam". There are to be no disputes "except over the better things".
  12. Muslims protect them from oppressors. If a Christian offends inadvertently, Muslims accompany him to the law-courts so that no more is exacted than God prescribes.
  13. Once the conditions are observed and the capitation tax paid, no Christian shall be tyrannized, and Christians shall not tyrannize Muslims.
  14. Muslims shall not take Christian women and maidens by force, "but only with the consent of their lords". The exception is women who freely choose to marry Muslims, "whether permanently or only for a time", out of "respect to the freewill of women".
  15. A Christian wife of a Muslim may continue in the Christian faith, attend church, and communicate with her spiritual advisers. She is not to be forced to forsake her faith.
  16. "Christians must attend to all repairs on their own churches, chapels and monasteries." If Muslims ask Christians for help, Christians shall not deny it.
  17. A Christian envoy sent to negotiate peace "between Moslems and Infidels" shall not be prevented. His service is accepted, and whoever despises him is among the wicked.
- **Provisions, part (c): commandments on Christians** (pp. 225–226).
  1. Give no aid to infidels, openly or secretly. Do not receive enemies of Muslims in their houses.
  2. Do not let enemy men stop at their houses or churches. Do not harbor enemy troops or aid them "with spear, arrow, sword or horse".
  3. Do not act as guides to them or show them how to ambush. Do not keep their possessions, communicate with them or shelter them "except only under duress".
  4. A Muslim at a Christian's house "may there be entertained three days and three nights; more than that is unnecessary."
  5. Christians shall avert from Muslims the abuse of tyrants. If they must hide a Muslim, they give him a place to lie and food.
  6. Muslim women and children shall not be betrayed or shown to the enemy.
  7. Violators are "loathsome to God". The treaty binds "until such time as God shall ordain."

  19. Muslims must help Christians repair churches and convents "not… with the aim of receiving any reward or debt".
  20. In war, Muslims will not employ any Christian "as a messenger, scout, guide, to show power, or for any other duty of war".
  21. Conditions on Christians: no spying, no sheltering of enemies, no halting places for enemies, no weapons, horses or men "or call for unnecessary things". "They must not annoy them and they should be honored as long as they persist in their religion and their care for their pact."
  22. They host Muslims "three days and three nights", with the same food.
  23. They hide a Muslim in need.
  24. Contraveners are cast out of the alliance. All are to abide by treaties "contracted with the monks".
  25. "The Messenger of Allah… must respect what he has granted… until the Hour of [Resurrection] arrives".
  26. "Whoever is unjust after this towards a [Christian] subject [dhimmi]… I will be his enemy on the Day of Judgment among all the Muslims."

- **Provisions:**

  2. "my horsemen, my foot-soldiers, my armies, my resources, and my Muslim partisans will protect the Christians as far away as they may be located", in peace or war.
  3. Protection of their persons, "churches, chapels, oratories, the monasteries of their monks, the residences of their anchorites", wherever they are, and of "their religion and their Church".
  4. He exempts them "for [from] any requisitions or any onerous obligations".
  5. "I forbid any conquerors of the faith to rule over them during their invasions or to oblige them to pay taxes unless they themselves willingly consent."
  6. No one may remove a bishop, monk or anchorite. No one may destroy their churches or use their buildings for mosques or Muslim homes.
  7. No capitation or tax on monks, bishops, or those who "wear woolen clothing or live alone in the mountains".
  8. "a limit set of four dirhams per year that all other Christians who are not clerics, monks, or hermits need to pay. Otherwise, let them provide one outfit of stripped [sic] material or one embroidered turban from Yemen. This is to help Muslims and to contribute to the growth of the Public Treasury. Were cloth difficult for them, they should provide its equivalent price, if they themselves willingly consent."
  9. The capitation on those with income, land, large commerce, mines or wealth may "not surpass, as a whole, twelve dirhams per year", and only on inhabitants and residents.
  10. Nothing is demanded of travellers or wayfarers. Land tax falls only on owners "of inherited properties over which the ruler has a right", without excess. The labor force is not overtaxed.
  11. Members of the alliance are not obliged to go to war. "No Christian is to be constrained to provide equipment to a single Muslims [sic], in money, in arm or in horses", unless freely, and those who give will be praised.
  12. "No Christian will be made Muslim by force: And dispute ye not with the People of the Book, except with means better [29:46]."
  13. Muslims help a Christian who commits a crime. They pardon him and seek reconciliation or compensation.
  14. Muslims must not abandon them. They share good and bad fortune.
  15. No forced marriages, and no taking of Christian girls against their parents' will.
  16. A Muslim husband respects his Christian wife's religion and her freedom to listen to her clerical superiors.
  17. Muslims help repair churches and convents "by pure donation".
  18. In war, no Christian is employed "as a messenger, scout, guide or spy".
  19. Conditions on Christians, introduced as "He is also placing conditions… and I demand…": no spying, no sheltering of enemies, no halting places for enemies, no weapons, horses or men for them, "nor must they treat them well".
  20. They host Muslims "for three days and three nights… with their animals", with the same food.
  21. They hide a Muslim in need. The text prints "guide him help" (*sic*; World III has "give him help").
  22. Contraveners are cast out. All abide by treaties with the monks and "every other commitment that each prophet has made with his nation". This holds "until the hour of the Resurrection, Allah-willing."

## 6. Internal inconsistencies and points to check

1. **Typo in a source year.** World IV is headed "Translation by John Andrew Morrow in 2103" (p. 237 [PDF 262]). Morrow's other translations here are dated 2013 (pp. 215, 233, 293).
2. **Sinai IV witness list.** The Skrobucha 1966 translation (p. 212) prints the same 16 anglicized names as Pococke 1809 (p. 206): "Homar, the son of Hattavi… Kastner the son of Abid…". Texts II, III, V and VI give a different list of 21–22 Companion names. Check whether Skrobucha's original has Pococke's list, or whether the book supplied it.
3. **Item division differs between I and IV:**
   - I: "Abombaker, the son of Anibi; Kaphe Ottoman, the son of Gafas" (p. 206).
   - IV: "Abombaker, the son of Ambi Kaphe; Ottoman, the son of Gafas" (p. 212).
4. **Who wrote the Sinai text (IV and VI).** Each version says both that Muhammad wrote it and that Ali wrote it in his own hand:
   - IV (p. 212): "this letter of protection, written by Muhammad, son of Abdullah" and "written in his own hand by Ali bin Abu Talib".
   - VI (pp. 216–217): the same pairing.
5. **Name of the Sinai scribe:**
   - II: "Aby-Taleb" only (p. 208).
   - III: "Aly ebn Taleb" (p. 210).
   - I, IV, V and VI: 'Ali ibn Abi Talib.
6. **CE date for the Sinai text.** Only II and III give a CE equivalent ("1st of August, 622"), for 3 Muharram AH 2 (pp. 208, 210). I, IV, V and VI give no CE date. Verifiers may wish to check the conversion; the book offers no others to compare.
7. **Sinai poll-tax ceiling:**
   - "twelve drachms": I (p. 206).
   - "twelve drachms of silver": II (p. 207).
   - "twelve silver drachmas": III (p. 209).
   - "ten dirhams a head a year": IV (p. 212).
   - "twelve Dirhams a head per year": V (p. 214).
   - "twelve dirhams a head per year": VI (p. 216).
8. **Qur'an citation for the same clause.** The "no strife with the People of the Book unless over what is for the good" clause is tagged [5:48] in VI (p. 216). IV gives no tag. The parallel clause in V and the quotation in I (XIII) are tagged [29:46].
9. **Sinai witness lists across II, III, V and VI:**
   - Count: II has 22 items, splitting "Thabet-ebn-Keys, MonKayetmeth". III has 21, with "Thabet ebn Kays Mou Khayetmeth" as one.
   - V and VI drop the "Thabet ebn Kays", "Mou Khayetmeth", "Hachem ebn Omyeh" and "Ebn el Ass" forms. In their place they have "Thabit Ibn Nafees/Nafis", "Zied/Zayd ibn Thabit", "Abou Hanifa Ibn Attaba / Bu Hanifah ibn 'Ubayyah" and "Hashim Ibn Obied / Hashim ibn 'Ubayyah".
   - Order: VI moves "Harith ibn Thabit; Abd al-Azim ibn Hasan" to positions 9–10. In V they are at 19–20.
   - Name forms that differ:
     - Father of Abd Allah: "Abdullah Ibn Omar Ibn El-Aas" (V) vs "Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As" (VI).
     - Last name: "Aamir Ibn Yasir" (V) vs "Amir ibn Yasin" (VI) vs "Amer-ebn-Yassin" (II).
     - "Abou Harirat" (V) vs "Abi Hurayrah" (VI).
     - "Talhat-ebn-Obeydallah" (II/III) vs "Talhah ibn Abd Allah" (VI).
     - Third witness's father: "Otman-ebn-Assan" (II) vs "Otman ebn Hassan" (III).
10. **Divergent renderings of the same Mauchin French (II vs III):**
    - "although these priests are cancelled by me" (II) vs "as those priests are my rayas" (III).
    - "it is forbidden to enter into discussion with them by soft measures" (II) vs "neither shall any enter into strife… but behave mildly towards them" (III).
11. **Church repair in the Sinai texts:**
    - I: "no person hinder them from repairing their churches". This is a ban on hindrance, not a promise of help.
    - II/III: "assisted to preserve their churches".
    - IV: "assisted in the improvement".
    - VI: "assisted in the maintenance".
    - V: "not be withheld from building churches or repairing convents".
12. **Christian wife (Sinai):**
    - Only V adds that the marriage "must not take place except after her consent".
    - II/III: she "go[es] to the house of / join[s] the Musulmans".
    - I: "marry a Muslim man".
13. **Church repair (Persia vs all other covenants).** Persia says "Christians must attend to all repairs on their own churches, chapels and monasteries" (p. 225). Every other covenant has Muslims help or not hinder.
14. **Issue date of the Covenant with the Christians of the World:**
    - I and II: "last day of the moon of the fourth month, the fourth year" (pp. 229, 232).
    - III: "the second day of the month of Rabi' Ashar during the fourth year" (p. 236).
    - The 1655 Latin (p. 291): "die Lunae ultimi mensis quarti, anni quarti". Check how "die Lunae" relates to the English "last day of the Moon".
    - IV (1538): no issue date at all.
    - Related texts: Persia "the Monday following the first four months of the Fourth Year" (p. 226); Assyrian "4th year of the Hegira" (p. 314).
15. **Scribe of the Covenant with the Christians of the World:**
    - I–III: Mu'awiyah ibn Abi Sufyan (pp. 229, 232, 236).
    - IV (1538): 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (p. 241).
    - Najran: Mu'awiyyah (p. 300).
    - Assyrian: "Moavijah Ben Sofian" (p. 314).
16. **Place in World II.** "the fourth year of the flight to Medina" (p. 232) makes Medina part of the era name, not the place of issue. World I and III say "in Medina".
17. **World III witness list:**
    - Mu'awiyyah ibn Abi Sufyan is both witness no. 5 and the scribe.
    - "Zayd ibn Arqam" is listed twice (nos. 28 and 35).
    - It splits "Abu Ahrifah; Ibn 'Usayr" into two. The 1655 Latin appears to read "Abu ahrifa ben-osair" as one name. The OCR is uncertain, so check this.
18. **World I/II vs III, all labelled [1630].** Rycaut and Van Dyck name only 4 witnesses plus "a number of / several others". Morrow's III names 38. The 1630/1655 facsimiles visibly carry a long list (pp. 289–290).
19. **World tax ceiling:**
    - I: "12 shillings yearly" (p. 228).
    - II: the sentence states no amount (p. 231).
    - III: "twelve dirhams" (p. 234).
    - IV: "twelve dirhams [fiddah]" (p. 239).
20. **Opposite senses (World I vs II).** I says violators are "a King, or any other of the faithful" (p. 227). II says "be he king, prince, or other unbeliever" (p. 230).
21. **World I vs II.** "Monk his profession" (I, p. 227) vs "nor any workman his profession" (II, p. 231).
22. **World IV vs III witness lists.** The lists differ substantially even where the names overlap:
    - "al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Malik" (IV) vs "Abd Allah ibn al-Abbas" and "Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib" (III).
    - "Sa'id ibn Mu'azz / Said ibn Ubadah" (IV) vs "Sa'd ibn Mu'adh / Sa'd ibn 'Ubadah" (III).
    - IV has "Farsus ibn Qasim ibn Badr ibn Ibrahim" where III has "Harfus ibn Zayd".
    - IV notes "[different handwriting]" after witness 20.
23. **Spelling within World IV.** "Abd al Muttalib" and "Abd al-Muktalib" appear on the same page (p. 237).
24. **1538 reproduction caption (p. 255).** The transliterated title reads "Muhammad ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib". The English gloss omits the second "ibn": "Muhammad ibn 'Abd Allah 'Abd al-Muttalib".
25. **1630 and 1655 reproduction captions.** Both carry the identical caption title "Testamentum et pactiones initae inter Mohamedem Apostolum Dei, et Christianae fedei cultores" (pp. 265, 281). The 1655 title page reproduced on p. 281 reads "…[sive] Testamentum, inter Muhamedem, et Christianae Religionis populos initum". The OCR also gives "fedei" (Latin *fidei*) in both captions; check whether this is a book typo.
26. **Najran sources and dating:**
    - The Arabic is headed "from the 9th century CE" (p. 301), but the English translation names no manuscript (p. 293).
    - The covenant has no date or place of issue. Its scribe line is only in brackets ("[Written by Mu'awiyyah ibn Abi Sufyan]", p. 300).
27. **Najran wording:**
    - The person shifts in "He is also placing conditions [upon the Christians] and I demand…" (p. 300).
    - "guide him help" (p. 300) vs "give him help" in World III/IV.
    - "to a single Muslims" (p. 299).
    - "one outfit of stripped material" (p. 298).
28. **Najran witness list vs World III/IV:**
    - "'Atiq ibn Abi Quhafah" for Abu Bakr.
    - "Thuniamah [Thumamah] ibn Qays" vs "Thabit ibn Qays".
    - "Hurqus ibn Zuhayr" vs "Harfus ibn Zayd".
    - "'Umar ibn Mazh'un 'Ammar" vs "'Uthman ibn Mat'un".
    - "Abu al-Ghaliyyah" vs "Abu al-Aliyyah".
    - "Abu Hudayfah" vs "Abu Ahrifah".
    - "Zayd ibn Thabit and his son 'Abd Allah" vs "Zayd ibn Thabit; Abd Allah ibn Zayd".
29. **Assyrian lists (p. 314):**
    - Malech's list (32 items) contains neither 'Uthman ibn 'Affan nor 'Ali ibn Abi Talib. The bracketed list (33) includes both.
    - Malech's forms map to quite different bracketed forms:
      - "Ebar Ibn Jaamir" → "Ammar ibn Yasir"
      - "Abu Harifah" → "Abu Hudayfah"
      - "Abubra" → "Abu Barah"
      - "Sabeh Ibn Kebis" → "Thabit ibn Qays"
      - "Jazid Ibn Sabib" → "Yazid ibn Thabit"
      - "Suhail Ibn Mifah" → "Sahl ibn Sufya [or Sifa]"
      - "Zaibar Ibn Aqam" → "Zubayr Awwam"
      - "Hasan Ibn Zabid" → "Hassan ibn Thabit"
      - "Abdullah Ibn Omar Alqazi" → "Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-Qadi"
      - "Kab Ibn Kab, Ibn Malech" → "Ka'b ibn Ka'b, Ka'b ibn Malik"
    - Malech has "Abdula, Ibn Masud" as two items; the bracketed list has one.
    - The bracketed list has "Su'd ibn Mu'adh" (typo).
30. **Assyrian tax unit.** It is dinars: one dinar, twelve dinars (p. 313). Every other covenant uses dirhams, drachms or shillings, except the Persian land tax ("four dinars").
31. **Assyrian wording.** "The tribute paid the Nazarenes shall be used to promote the teachings of Islam" (p. 313). The direction of payment is ambiguous as printed.
32. **Haddad's opening.** "Whenever monies, devotees and pilgrims gather" (p. 213). Probably "monks", but it could be OCR or a book typo; check.
33. **Wording of source lines.** Sinai VI: "Translated by… Morrow in 2013". World III: "Translation cited by… Morrow in 2013". World IV and Najran: "Translation by… Morrow".
34. **Chapter 8 Arabic heading.** It gives no manuscript, date or source for the "Corrected Arabic Original" (p. 218). The 1638 Turkish plate (p. 222) is the only document dated in Chapter 8 apart from the in-text dates.
